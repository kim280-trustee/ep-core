# Social Studies P1–M6 — Teacher Implementation Guide

**Curriculum:** TH-BASIC-CORE-2008-2017  
**Learning area:** Social Studies, Religion and Culture  
**Coverage:** 418 unique indicators; 540 required grade alignments  
**Status:** Teacher planning guidance; source packs remain drafts until the consolidated application import test.

## 1. How to use the packs

1. Select the pack for the class grade. For M4, M5 or M6, use the shared M4–M6 pack; the importer expands each of its 61 outcomes to all three aligned grades.
2. Locate the lesson by its official objective code and check that the Thai indicator wording matches the intended lesson.
3. Read the strand-specific learning intention, four-step teaching sequence, resource list and differentiation note before teaching.
4. Use the practical activity to model the skill or knowledge. Select the independent-practice questions that best reveal individual understanding.
5. Review the expected evidence and teacher check. Give feedback against the indicator, not against memorised wording alone.
6. Record whether the learner has demonstrated the indicator, needs guided practice, or needs a different explanation or resource. Do not treat one formative response as a final high-stakes grade.

The packs provide three answer-keyed formative tasks for every unique indicator. They are classroom learning checks, not standardized examinations. For open-ended tasks, the answer guidance describes acceptable evidence and reasoning; it is not a single mandatory sentence.

## 2. A practical lesson structure

Adapt the timing to the school's timetable. For a 50-minute lesson, this sequence is a useful starting point:

| Stage | Suggested time | Teacher and learner actions |
|---|---:|---|
| Connect | 5 minutes | Introduce a familiar place, situation, question, object, image or short source linked to the indicator. |
| Teach and model | 10 minutes | Explain key vocabulary and model how to use the relevant source, map, case, timeline or decision tool. |
| Guided practice | 15 minutes | Learners practise in pairs or groups; teacher checks misconceptions and asks learners to explain their evidence. |
| Independent evidence | 15 minutes | Learners complete an individual question, short explanation, map, timeline, decision or practical response. |
| Exit check | 5 minutes | Ask learners to state one thing they can now demonstrate and one point they still need help with. |

Do not force every lesson into exactly 50 minutes. Practical, discussion-based or source-analysis lessons may need more time.

## 3. Strand-specific teaching and evidence

### SO1 — Religion, ethics and ways of life

Use balanced, age-appropriate information about traditions, teachings, values, practices and their historical or social contexts. Make clear whether a statement is a source fact, a tradition's own account, or an individual's interpretation. Invite respectful comparison without ranking religions or asking learners to disclose private belief.

Evidence may include identifying a teaching or practice, explaining its context, interpreting a source, or applying a shared ethical principle to a situation. **Never assess a learner's personal faith, religious identity, worship, prayer or willingness to participate in a religious activity.** Offer an equivalent non-devotional way to demonstrate the same curriculum understanding.

### SO2 — Civics, rights and responsibilities

Use realistic school, family, community and public-life cases. Identify stakeholders, rights, responsibilities, relevant rules and appropriate civic processes. Distinguish a legal rule from a school expectation or a personal preference. Ask learners to justify how a response treats affected people fairly.

Evidence may include a stakeholder map, rights-and-duties chart, explanation of a process, or a justified solution to a case.

### SO3 — Economics and resource decisions

Use familiar examples involving needs and wants, limited resources, work, production, goods, services, money, saving, spending and choices. State any numbers, prices or assumptions in a case so learners are not expected to guess. Where calculations are involved, assess both the calculation and the economic reasoning.

Evidence may include a simple budget, comparison table, resource-flow diagram, choice explanation or recommendation. Avoid presenting classroom examples as personal financial advice.

### SO4 — History and historical evidence

Use age-appropriate primary and secondary sources, dated images, artefact records, timelines and accounts from more than one perspective where appropriate. Teach learners to distinguish a source's direct information from an inference, opinion or later interpretation. Check dates, sequence, context and whether the evidence supports the conclusion.

Evidence may include a timeline, source annotation, comparison of accounts, cause-and-consequence explanation or a short evidence-based interpretation.

### SO5 — Geography and human–environment relationships

Use maps, atlases, keys, scale, photographs, local observations and simple data. Teach learners to identify location and pattern before explaining relationships. Check that map symbols, direction, scale and data are interpreted correctly. Link proposed actions to the evidence rather than accepting unsupported suggestions.

Evidence may include an annotated map, a description of a spatial pattern, a comparison of places, an explanation of a human–environment relationship or a practical response to an environmental issue.

## 4. Grade-band differentiation

| Grade band | Useful scaffolds | Appropriate evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1–P3 | Concrete objects, large pictures, short instructions, oral modelling, sorting and drawing | Pointing, matching, labelled drawing, oral explanation and simple choices |
| P4–P6 | Short written cases, labelled visuals, vocabulary frames and simple organisers | Short explanations, tables, timelines, annotated maps and justified choices |
| M1–M3 | Guided source reading, subject vocabulary, comparison frames and structured discussion | Evidence-based paragraphs, source comparisons, case analysis and reasoned solutions |
| M4–M6 | Multiple sources, data, competing viewpoints, independent research and evaluation | Supported interpretations, evaluation of alternatives, recommendations and presentations |

Reduce language load without reducing the Social Studies learning target. A learner may explain orally, draw, annotate a map or use a structured response when that format measures the same indicator. Do not confuse English-language proficiency with Social Studies understanding.

## 5. Formative assessment rubric

Use this common rubric alongside the lesson's own expected-evidence statement. The indicator-specific evidence takes priority when it requires a particular skill or source.

| Dimension | Not yet demonstrated | Developing | Demonstrated |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy | Response is missing, unrelated or substantially inaccurate | Some relevant facts or concepts are correct, but there are gaps or misconceptions | Key facts, concepts or processes match the indicator |
| Explanation and evidence | Gives an unsupported statement or cannot explain the response | Gives a partial reason or refers to evidence without explaining the link | Explains the idea and uses a relevant example, source, map or case detail |
| Application or transfer | Cannot yet apply the learning to the task or situation | Applies part of the learning with prompts or support | Applies the learning accurately and justifies an appropriate response |

Record the dimension that needs support rather than assigning a low overall judgement because of one language or writing difficulty. Give a specific next step, such as identifying the relevant source detail, correcting a map key, explaining a right or duty, or comparing the consequences of two choices.

## 6. Feedback and follow-up

- **Accuracy feedback:** identify the fact, concept, date, rule, map feature or economic relationship that needs correction.
- **Evidence feedback:** ask which source detail, map feature, case fact or example supports the learner's claim.
- **Reasoning feedback:** ask the learner to explain how the evidence leads to the conclusion.
- **Application feedback:** ask the learner to try the same principle in a new but related situation.
- **Re-teaching:** use a different source, model, visual or worked example; do not simply repeat the same explanation or assign more copying.

A learner who has not yet demonstrated an indicator should receive another opportunity using a suitable scaffold. A learner who has demonstrated it can apply the same knowledge to a less familiar case or more complex source.

## 7. Source quality, inclusion and classroom safety

- Use reliable, age-appropriate sources and identify their author, publisher, date and context when relevant.
- Do not invent historical quotations, legal rules, statistics, map data or religious teachings. Check current legal and civic information against authoritative Thai sources before using it as a factual example.
- Avoid stereotypes about religions, ethnic or cultural communities, regions, occupations, income groups or countries.
- Do not require learners to reveal family income, religion, political views, migration status or other private information to complete a task.
- Use fictional or anonymised cases when a real example could expose or embarrass a learner or family.
- Provide accessible text, visuals and oral explanations where possible. Check that images and maps are legible and that tasks do not depend on a learner owning a device or buying materials.
- For sensitive discussion, establish respectful listening rules and intervene when comments target a person or group.

## 8. Shared M4–M6 outcomes and importing

The 61 upper-secondary M46 objective codes are shared outcomes aligned to M4, M5 and M6. They are not 61 separate indicators for each grade. The teacher importer expands each shared outcome to three grade-specific draft lesson rows and uses grade-scoped content and question codes to prevent collisions.

The importer must resolve each shared objective against all three grade alignments. Do not manually change M46 to M4, M5 or M6 in the source pack. Do not import a pack into a live school until the consolidated import and end-to-end application test has been completed.

## 9. Curriculum classification reminders

- `must_know` and `should_know` are priority classifications from the 2021 OBEC source.
- `between_learning` and `terminal` are pathway classifications from the OBEC indicator pathway document.
- These classifications describe different things and must not be substituted for one another.
- All 540 Social Studies grade alignments are required in the national master catalogue. A Should-Know label does not make an official indicator optional for curriculum coverage.
