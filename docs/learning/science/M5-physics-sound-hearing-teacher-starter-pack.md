# M5 Physics — Sound, Hearing and Sound Phenomena
## Official alignment
Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017), Additional Physics, M5 outcomes 6–7. IPST Physics curriculum-use manual, printed pp. 136 and 138.

## Outcome 6 — Sound intensity, sound level and hearing
**Learning objective:** Explain sound intensity and sound level, their relationship to source power and distance, the role of frequency and the ear in hearing, sound quality, and the effects and prevention of noise pollution; calculate sound intensity and sound level.

**Lesson starter (10 minutes):** Play two short, safe audio samples at low volume. Ask students what makes one sound seem louder or higher-pitched. Introduce intensity, decibels, frequency, hearing range and sound quality. Never expose students to loud sound.

**Teach and practise:** Distinguish intensity (power per unit area) from sound level (logarithmic measure in dB); use supplied datasets to compare distance and source power; identify ear structures and the effect of prolonged exposure; investigate noise pollution and prevention.

**Answer-keyed questions**
1. A source emits 2 W uniformly through an area of 4 m². Find intensity. **Answer:** I = P/A = 0.5 W m⁻².
2. If intensity is 10⁻⁶ W m⁻² and the reference intensity is 10⁻¹² W m⁻², find sound level. **Answer:** β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀) = 60 dB.
3. Give two ways to reduce harmful noise exposure. **Answer:** Reduce source volume/duration; increase distance or use suitable hearing protection.

## Outcome 7 — Resonance, beats, standing waves and Doppler effect
**Learning objective:** Investigate resonance of air in a pipe open at one end; explain beats, standing sound waves, the Doppler effect and sonic shock waves; calculate related quantities and describe practical applications.

**Lesson starter (10 minutes):** Use a simulation or low-volume audio demonstration to compare two nearby frequencies. Students describe the repeating rise and fall in loudness, then connect it to beats.

**Teach and practise:** Model the fundamental and harmonics in an open-closed pipe; explain beats as the difference between two close frequencies; describe nodes and antinodes in standing waves; use diagrams to explain apparent frequency change when source/listener move; distinguish a sonic boom from ordinary sound.

**Answer-keyed questions**
1. Two tones have frequencies 256 Hz and 260 Hz. Find beat frequency. **Answer:** 4 Hz.
2. An open-closed pipe has length 0.25 m and sound speed 340 m s⁻¹. Find its fundamental frequency using f = v/(4L). **Answer:** 340 Hz.
3. What causes the Doppler effect? **Answer:** Relative motion of source and observer changes the observed frequency.

## Safety and assessment
Use simulations, diagrams and low-volume recordings. Do not use high-volume sound, expose students to noise hazards, or conduct unsafe demonstrations. Assess with labelled wave diagrams, short calculations and explanations using evidence.
