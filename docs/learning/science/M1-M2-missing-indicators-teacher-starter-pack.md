# M1–M2 Science and Technology — Missing Official Indicator Teacher Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised 2017  
**Source:** OBEC / IPST, *Indicators and Core Learning Content: Must-Know and Should-Know* (2021), M1 pages 104–107; M2 pages 124–126.  
**Scope:** 24 indicators added in this batch: M1 technology 9; M2 matter 6; M2 design and technology 5; M2 computing 4.  
**Format:** One lesson starter and three answer-keyed formative questions per indicator. These are teacher planning resources, not published app content.

## M1 — Design and Technology (W4.1)

### M1-S4_1-01 — Explain technology in everyday life

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Map a familiar object as input → process → output; add feedback that improves it.

**Formative questions**
1. What is technology? **Answer:** A product or method people create to meet needs or solve problems.
2. What are the three main parts of a simple technology system? **Answer:** Input, process and output.
3. Give one example of feedback. **Answer:** A thermostat switches heating off when the target temperature is reached.

### M1-S4_1-02 — Identify a community problem or need

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Observe a school or community situation; write a clear problem statement and list reliable information sources.

**Formative questions**
1. Give one example of a community need. **Answer:** A safer pedestrian crossing near school.
2. Why define the problem before designing? **Answer:** To understand what must be solved and for whom.
3. What is intellectual property? **Answer:** A person's legally protected creation or work.

### M1-S4_1-03 — Design and plan a solution

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Teams compare two solutions to a local problem, sketch the best one and create a simple task timeline.

**Formative questions**
1. Name two ways to show a design. **Answer:** A sketch and a flowchart.
2. Why compare more than one solution? **Answer:** To choose the option that best fits the needs and limits.
3. What should a plan include? **Answer:** Tasks, resources, responsibilities and time.

### M1-S4_1-04 — Test and improve a solution

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Test a paper bridge with equal weights, record results, identify a weakness and revise the design.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a test criterion? **Answer:** A condition used to judge whether a solution works.
2. Why record test results? **Answer:** To use evidence when improving the design.
3. What should happen after a weakness is found? **Answer:** Modify the design and test again.

### M1-S4_1-05 — Choose and use tools safely

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Match materials and tools to a simple classroom model; practise safe handling and storage.

**Formative questions**
1. Why choose material based on its properties? **Answer:** Different materials have different strength, flexibility and other properties.
2. Name one electrical component used in a simple model. **Answer:** An LED or buzzer.
3. Give one tool-safety rule. **Answer:** Use the tool as instructed and store it safely.


## M1 — Computational Thinking (W4.2)

### M1-S4_2-01 — Design an algorithm using abstraction

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Break a school-day task into essential steps; ignore irrelevant details and draw a flowchart.

**Formative questions**
1. What is an algorithm? **Answer:** A clear sequence of steps to solve a problem.
2. What does abstraction mean? **Answer:** Focusing on important details and ignoring unnecessary ones.
3. Name one way to represent an algorithm. **Answer:** A flowchart or pseudocode.

### M1-S4_2-02 — Write a simple program

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Use Scratch or pseudocode to calculate an area using a variable and a condition; test sample inputs.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a variable? **Answer:** A named place to store a value.
2. What does an IF condition do? **Answer:** Chooses an action based on whether a test is true.
3. Why test a program with different inputs? **Answer:** To find errors and check expected results.

### M1-S4_2-03 — Collect and present data digitally

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Collect anonymous class preference data, calculate totals or averages and create a chart.

**Formative questions**
1. What is primary data? **Answer:** Data collected directly for a task.
2. Why process data? **Answer:** To make it useful and meaningful.
3. Name one suitable way to present data. **Answer:** A bar chart or table.

### M1-S4_2-04 — Use information technology safely

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Review example websites and messages; identify privacy risks, unsuitable content and source-use rules.

**Formative questions**
1. Name one way to protect an online account. **Answer:** Use a strong unique password and keep it private.
2. What should you do before sharing a photo of someone? **Answer:** Get their permission.
3. What does a Creative Commons licence explain? **Answer:** How a work may be reused under stated conditions.


## M2 — Matter and Solutions (W2.1)

### M2-S2_1-01 — Explain methods for separating mixtures

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Demonstrate with diagrams how evaporation, crystallisation, distillation, paper chromatography and solvent extraction separate mixtures.

**Formative questions**
1. Which method can recover salt from salt water? **Answer:** Evaporation.
2. Which method separates liquids with different boiling points? **Answer:** Simple distillation.
3. What does paper chromatography separate? **Answer:** Components of a mixture based on their movement through paper and solvent.

### M2-S2_1-02 — Separate mixtures using suitable methods

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Use safe teacher-prepared mixtures to select and demonstrate filtration, evaporation or chromatography; record observations.

**Formative questions**
1. Which method separates sand from water? **Answer:** Filtration.
2. Which method separates coloured dyes? **Answer:** Paper chromatography.
3. Why must the method match the mixture? **Answer:** Separation depends on differences in physical properties.

### M2-S2_1-03 — Apply separation methods to daily problems

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Design a method to recover clean water or separate a useful substance from a mixture; explain the choice.

**Formative questions**
1. Which separation method could remove insoluble dirt from water? **Answer:** Filtration.
2. What should be considered when choosing a method? **Answer:** Safety, cost, time and properties of the mixture.
3. How can mathematics help? **Answer:** By measuring quantities, time or recovery rate.

### M2-S2_1-04 — Investigate factors affecting solubility

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Plan a controlled investigation of temperature or solute type and compare the amount dissolved; discuss pressure in gases.

**Formative questions**
1. What is solubility? **Answer:** The amount of solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a stated condition.
2. Name one factor affecting the solubility of many solids. **Answer:** Temperature.
3. How can a fair test be maintained? **Answer:** Change one variable and keep others constant.

### M2-S2_1-05 — Calculate concentration as a percentage

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Solve simple percentage concentration examples using labelled classroom-safe solutions and units.

**Formative questions**
1. What does 5% mass/volume mean? **Answer:** 5 g of solute in 100 mL of solution.
2. What does percentage volume/volume compare? **Answer:** Solute volume with total solution volume.
3. Why include units? **Answer:** To show what quantities are being compared.

### M2-S2_1-06 — Use solutions safely in everyday life

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Compare product labels and discuss why dilution instructions and concentration matter; do not mix household chemicals.

**Formative questions**
1. Why follow a dilution instruction? **Answer:** To use the correct concentration safely.
2. Can household cleaning products be mixed freely? **Answer:** No; mixing can release dangerous substances.
3. Give one everyday use of concentration knowledge. **Answer:** Preparing a correctly diluted solution.


## M2 — Design and Technology (W4.1)

### M2-S4_1-01 — Predict technology trends and impacts

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Compare two technologies used in farming or transport and predict likely benefits and risks.

**Formative questions**
1. Name one factor that changes technology. **Answer:** New scientific knowledge or changing human needs.
2. Give one possible environmental impact. **Answer:** More electronic waste or lower fuel use.
3. What should guide technology choice? **Answer:** Suitability and its effects on people, society and the environment.

### M2-S4_1-02 — Define a local problem or need

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Interview or observe a school/community user; define the need and collect relevant evidence.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a problem scope? **Answer:** A clear statement of what is included in the problem.
2. Why gather information from users? **Answer:** To understand the real need.
3. Name one reliable source. **Answer:** Direct observation, an interview or a trusted report.

### M2-S4_1-03 — Design and plan a solution within constraints

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Compare design options against a budget, time and available materials; draw a plan and timeline.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a design constraint? **Answer:** A limit such as cost, time or available materials.
2. Why compare options? **Answer:** To choose the most suitable solution.
3. What does a project timeline show? **Answer:** Tasks and when they should be completed.

### M2-S4_1-04 — Evaluate and improve a designed solution

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Test a prototype against agreed criteria; record defects, make changes and present evidence.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a prototype? **Answer:** An early model used to test a design.
2. What is evaluation? **Answer:** Judging performance against criteria.
3. Why retest after an improvement? **Answer:** To check whether the change solved the problem.

### M2-S4_1-05 — Use materials and mechanisms safely

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Inspect safe classroom tools and low-voltage components; choose suitable materials and demonstrate safe use.

**Formative questions**
1. Why choose a material for its properties? **Answer:** To ensure it is suitable for the task.
2. Name one mechanism. **Answer:** A gear, pulley or wheel-and-axle.
3. What should be done with a damaged electrical tool? **Answer:** Stop using it and report it to the teacher.


## M2 — Computational Thinking (W4.2)

### M2-S4_2-01 — Design a computational algorithm

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Plan steps for sorting classroom records or finding the shortest route; represent decisions and repetition.

**Formative questions**
1. What is computational thinking? **Answer:** Solving problems using logical steps and useful representations.
2. What does a loop do? **Answer:** Repeats instructions.
3. What is a decision in a flowchart? **Answer:** A point where a condition selects a path.

### M2-S4_2-02 — Write a program using logic and functions

**Priority:** must know  
**Activity:** Write pseudocode or a short program using Boolean conditions and a reusable function; test edge cases.

**Formative questions**
1. What is a Boolean value? **Answer:** True or false.
2. What is a function? **Answer:** A named reusable block of instructions.
3. Why use a function? **Answer:** To reuse code and organise a program.

### M2-S4_2-03 — Explain computer systems and communication technology

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Label input, processing, storage and output components; trace how a message travels across a network.

**Formative questions**
1. Name one input device. **Answer:** A keyboard or microphone.
2. What is a network? **Answer:** Connected devices that exchange data.
3. Why use communication protocols? **Answer:** They set shared rules for exchanging information.

### M2-S4_2-04 — Use information technology responsibly

**Priority:** should know  
**Activity:** Review a sample digital project; add creator credit, licence information and safe-sharing choices.

**Formative questions**
1. What does copyright protect? **Answer:** Original creative work.
2. Why credit a source? **Answer:** To acknowledge the creator and show where information came from.
3. What should you do with harmful online content? **Answer:** Do not forward it; report it to a trusted adult or responsible service.

## Teacher-use notes

- Use school-approved materials and teacher supervision for practical activities.
- Do not mix household chemicals; use only labelled, teacher-prepared solutions.
- Use low-voltage classroom components only. Never connect student-built circuits to mains electricity.
- Keep learner data anonymous in classroom examples and respect privacy, copyright and source licences.
- Review lesson language and local suitability before importing through the existing Teacher Content Create / Question Bank workflows.
