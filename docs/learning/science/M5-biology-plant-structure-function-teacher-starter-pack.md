# M5 Biology — Plant Structure and Function Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017), Additional Biology strand 3  
**Subject:** BIOLOGY  
**Grade:** M5  
**Coverage:** 18 official M5 learning outcomes. Strand 3 has no M4 outcomes in the official curriculum; this batch is correctly aligned to M5 (pp. 110–111).

Each lesson aligns to one official learning outcome and includes three practice questions with answer keys. Use prepared slides, diagrams, published datasets and teacher-supervised plant investigations. Do not ask students to ingest plant materials or handle unknown species.

## Lesson 1 — Classify plant tissues

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-01`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 1  
**Learning focus:** Explain meristematic and permanent tissues, including dermal, ground and vascular tissues.

**Practice**
1. What is a meristem?  
   **Answer:** A region of actively dividing plant cells.
2. Name the two main vascular tissues.  
   **Answer:** Xylem and phloem.
3. What is one role of epidermal tissue?  
   **Answer:** Protection and regulation of exchange.

## Lesson 2 — Compare internal root structures

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-02`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 2  
**Learning focus:** Observe monocot and dicot root cross-sections and identify epidermis, cortex, endodermis and vascular tissues.

**Practice**
1. What is the function of root hairs?  
   **Answer:** They increase surface area for absorption.
2. Where is the endodermis found?  
   **Answer:** At the inner boundary of the root cortex.
3. How do typical monocot and dicot roots differ internally?  
   **Answer:** Their vascular arrangements differ; dicot roots commonly have central star-shaped xylem, while monocot roots commonly have a central pith with vascular tissues around it.

## Lesson 3 — Compare internal stem structures

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-03`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 3  
**Learning focus:** Compare monocot and dicot stem cross-sections and relate vascular-bundle arrangement to stem organisation.

**Practice**
1. What do vascular bundles contain?  
   **Answer:** Xylem and phloem.
2. How are vascular bundles commonly arranged in a dicot stem?  
   **Answer:** In a ring.
3. How are they commonly arranged in a monocot stem?  
   **Answer:** Scattered through the ground tissue.

## Lesson 4 — Explain the internal structure of leaves

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-04`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 4  
**Learning focus:** Identify epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, stomata and veins in a leaf cross-section.

**Practice**
1. Which tissue is usually rich in chloroplasts?  
   **Answer:** Palisade mesophyll.
2. What is the role of stomata?  
   **Answer:** They regulate gas exchange and water vapour loss.
3. What do leaf veins contain?  
   **Answer:** Xylem and phloem.

## Lesson 5 — Explain plant gas exchange and transpiration

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-05`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 5  
**Learning focus:** Investigate stomatal gas exchange and transpiration and interpret the effects of environmental conditions.

**Practice**
1. What is transpiration?  
   **Answer:** Loss of water vapour from aerial parts of a plant, mainly through stomata.
2. Which gas enters leaves for photosynthesis?  
   **Answer:** Carbon dioxide.
3. Name one factor that can increase transpiration.  
   **Answer:** Higher air movement, temperature or light intensity, or lower humidity.

## Lesson 6 — Explain water and mineral transport

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-06`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 6  
**Learning focus:** Explain water uptake and movement through xylem, including cohesion, adhesion and transpiration pull.

**Practice**
1. Which tissue transports water upward?  
   **Answer:** Xylem.
2. What is transpiration pull?  
   **Answer:** A tension created by water evaporation from leaves that helps draw water upward.
3. How do root hairs help water uptake?  
   **Answer:** They provide a large surface area for absorption.

## Lesson 7 — Explain mineral nutrition in plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-07`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 7  
**Learning focus:** Relate essential mineral elements to plant growth and recognise common deficiency symptoms.

**Practice**
1. Which mineral is needed to make chlorophyll?  
   **Answer:** Magnesium.
2. Why do plants need nitrogen?  
   **Answer:** To make amino acids, proteins and nucleic acids.
3. Name one possible symptom of mineral deficiency.  
   **Answer:** Chlorosis, stunted growth or poor root development, depending on the element.

## Lesson 8 — Explain translocation of food in plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-08`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 8  
**Learning focus:** Explain source-to-sink movement of sugars through phloem and the pressure-flow model.

**Practice**
1. Which tissue transports sugars?  
   **Answer:** Phloem.
2. What is a source in translocation?  
   **Answer:** A plant region that supplies sugars, such as a photosynthesising leaf.
3. What is a sink?  
   **Answer:** A region that uses or stores sugars, such as a growing root or fruit.

## Lesson 9 — Review historical experiments on photosynthesis

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-09`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 9  
**Learning focus:** Compare classic experiments and the evidence they provided about light, carbon dioxide, oxygen and chlorophyll.

**Practice**
1. What did van Helmont investigate?  
   **Answer:** He studied plant mass change and concluded that most added plant mass did not come from soil.
2. What did Priestley’s experiments help demonstrate?  
   **Answer:** Plants can restore air that has been altered by combustion or animal respiration.
3. What did Engelmann’s experiment indicate?  
   **Answer:** Photosynthesis is most effective at wavelengths absorbed by chlorophyll, especially red and blue light.

## Lesson 10 — Explain photosynthesis in C3 plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-10`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 10  
**Learning focus:** Describe light-dependent reactions and the Calvin cycle and relate their inputs and outputs.

**Practice**
1. Where do light-dependent reactions occur?  
   **Answer:** Thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.
2. What is produced by the light-dependent reactions for the Calvin cycle?  
   **Answer:** ATP and NADPH.
3. What is the main carbon-fixation enzyme in the Calvin cycle?  
   **Answer:** RuBisCO.

## Lesson 11 — Compare carbon fixation in C3 C4 and CAM plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-11`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 11  
**Learning focus:** Compare initial carbon fixation, spatial or temporal separation and environmental adaptations in C3, C4 and CAM plants.

**Practice**
1. What is the first stable product in C3 carbon fixation?  
   **Answer:** A three-carbon compound, 3-phosphoglycerate.
2. How do C4 plants reduce photorespiration?  
   **Answer:** They initially fix carbon dioxide into four-carbon compounds in mesophyll cells and concentrate carbon dioxide near Rubisco in bundle-sheath cells.
3. When do CAM plants usually open stomata?  
   **Answer:** At night, reducing water loss in dry environments.

## Lesson 12 — Analyse factors affecting photosynthesis

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-12`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 12  
**Learning focus:** Use controlled data to analyse how light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature affect photosynthesis rate.

**Practice**
1. What is a limiting factor?  
   **Answer:** A factor in shortest supply relative to demand that restricts the rate of a process.
2. Why can increasing light stop increasing photosynthesis rate?  
   **Answer:** Another factor becomes limiting or the photosynthetic system reaches saturation.
3. How can temperature affect photosynthesis?  
   **Answer:** Rate rises to an optimum then may fall as enzyme activity is disrupted at higher temperatures.

## Lesson 13 — Explain alternation of generations in flowering plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-13`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 13  
**Learning focus:** Trace the dominant sporophyte and reduced gametophyte stages in the flowering-plant life cycle.

**Practice**
1. Which generation is dominant in flowering plants?  
   **Answer:** The diploid sporophyte.
2. What does meiosis produce in the anther and ovule?  
   **Answer:** Haploid spores.
3. What develops from a microspore?  
   **Answer:** A pollen grain, the male gametophyte.

## Lesson 14 — Compare male and female gamete formation in flowering plants

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-14`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 14  
**Learning focus:** Compare pollen development, embryo-sac formation, pollination and double fertilisation.

**Practice**
1. Where are pollen grains produced?  
   **Answer:** In the anthers.
2. Where does the embryo sac develop?  
   **Answer:** Within the ovule in the ovary.
3. What is double fertilisation?  
   **Answer:** One sperm fertilises the egg to form a zygote; another fuses with the polar nuclei to form endosperm.

## Lesson 15 — Explain seed and fruit development

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-15`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 15  
**Learning focus:** Relate fertilisation to seed and fruit formation and identify major seed structures.

**Practice**
1. What does the ovule usually become?  
   **Answer:** A seed.
2. What does the ovary usually become?  
   **Answer:** A fruit.
3. What is the function of the seed coat?  
   **Answer:** It protects the embryo and internal seed tissues.

## Lesson 16 — Investigate seed germination and dormancy

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-16`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 16  
**Learning focus:** Design safe investigations into water, oxygen, temperature and dormancy-breaking conditions.

**Practice**
1. What three conditions are commonly needed for germination?  
   **Answer:** Water, oxygen and a suitable temperature.
2. What is seed dormancy?  
   **Answer:** A state in which a viable seed does not germinate even when some favourable conditions are present.
3. Give one method that may break dormancy in suitable species.  
   **Answer:** Scarification, stratification or light/temperature treatment, depending on the species.

## Lesson 17 — Explain the roles of plant hormones

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-17`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 17  
**Learning focus:** Compare auxin, cytokinin, gibberellin, ethylene and abscisic acid and evaluate agricultural uses.

**Practice**
1. Which hormone promotes cell elongation in shoots?  
   **Answer:** Auxin.
2. Which hormone is strongly associated with fruit ripening?  
   **Answer:** Ethylene.
3. Which hormone commonly promotes stomatal closure during water stress?  
   **Answer:** Abscisic acid.

## Lesson 18 — Investigate plant responses to external stimuli

**Objective:** `M5-BIO-PLANT-18`  
**Official alignment:** Additional Biology strand 3, M5 learning outcome 18  
**Learning focus:** Investigate phototropism, gravitropism, thigmotropism and hydrotropism using controlled observations.

**Practice**
1. What is phototropism?  
   **Answer:** Directional plant growth in response to light.
2. What is gravitropism?  
   **Answer:** Directional growth in response to gravity.
3. How can a tendril respond to touch?  
   **Answer:** It can coil around a support through thigmotropic growth.
