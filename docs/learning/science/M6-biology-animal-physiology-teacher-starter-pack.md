# M6 Biology Teacher Starter Pack — Nervous Coordination, Movement, Reproduction and Behaviour

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017), Additional Biology, strand 4  
**Grade:** M6  
**Coverage:** 19 official learning outcomes; 19 lesson starters; 57 practice questions with answer keys  
**Official alignment:** OBEC/IPST revised curriculum, pp. 113–114  
**Use:** Teacher planning resource. Review scientific accuracy, language level and safeguarding requirements before adapting or publishing.

## Teaching and safeguarding guidance

Use diagrams, models, simulations, prepared datasets and age-appropriate scientific resources. Sensory activities must be optional, accessible and safe; do not use sharp objects, extreme temperatures, loud sounds, food allergens or anything that could cause pain. Reproductive biology should be taught with respectful, medically accurate language and without asking learners to disclose personal experiences. Avoid animal handling or experiments that stress animals. Refer personal medical questions to qualified health professionals.

## Lesson sequence and practice

### 1. Nervous systems across animal groups
**Objective:** Compare nervous-system organisation in Hydra, Planaria, earthworms, prawns, snails, insects and vertebrates.  
**Starter:** Display nervous-system diagrams from the listed groups; learners arrange them from diffuse nerve nets to more centralised systems.

1. What type of nervous organisation is found in Hydra?  
   **Answer:** A diffuse nerve net.
2. What is a major feature of the Planaria nervous system?  
   **Answer:** Longitudinal nerve cords connected by transverse nerves, often described as a ladder-like arrangement.
3. What is the main advantage of centralisation in a nervous system?  
   **Answer:** It supports coordination and integration of information from different body regions.

### 2. Neuron structure and function
**Objective:** Identify neuron structures and relate them to signal transmission.  
**Starter:** Label a neuron diagram and trace the direction of a signal from input region to output region.

1. What is the usual role of dendrites?  
   **Answer:** They receive signals from other cells.
2. What is the function of the axon?  
   **Answer:** It carries electrical impulses away from the cell body.
3. What is the role of myelin on many axons?  
   **Answer:** It insulates the axon and increases the speed of impulse conduction.

### 3. Membrane potential and nerve impulses
**Objective:** Explain resting potential, action potentials and synaptic transmission.  
**Starter:** Use an animation or graph to identify resting state, depolarisation, repolarisation and recovery.

1. What is the resting membrane potential?  
   **Answer:** The electrical potential difference across a neuron's membrane when it is not producing an action potential.
2. What causes depolarisation during a typical neuronal action potential?  
   **Answer:** Voltage-gated sodium channels open and sodium ions enter the neuron.
3. How is a signal commonly passed across a chemical synapse?  
   **Answer:** Neurotransmitters are released from the presynaptic terminal and bind to receptors on the next cell.

### 4. Central and peripheral nervous systems
**Objective:** Distinguish the central nervous system (CNS) from the peripheral nervous system (PNS).  
**Starter:** Sort labelled structures into CNS and PNS and draw pathways linking receptors, CNS and effectors.

1. Which structures make up the CNS?  
   **Answer:** The brain and spinal cord.
2. What does the PNS include?  
   **Answer:** Nerves and ganglia outside the brain and spinal cord.
3. What is one role of sensory neurons?  
   **Answer:** They carry information from receptors towards the CNS.

### 5. Brain and spinal cord
**Objective:** Relate major brain regions and spinal cord structures to their functions.  
**Starter:** Annotate a brain diagram with broad functions, then trace a simple reflex pathway through the spinal cord.

1. Which brain region is important for planning, memory and conscious thought?  
   **Answer:** The forebrain, particularly the cerebral hemispheres.
2. Which structure helps coordinate balance and movement?  
   **Answer:** The cerebellum, part of the hindbrain.
3. Give one important function of the spinal cord.  
   **Answer:** It carries signals between the brain and body and coordinates some reflexes.

### 6. Somatic and autonomic nervous systems
**Objective:** Compare somatic and autonomic control.  
**Starter:** Classify example responses as mainly somatic or autonomic.

1. Which system controls skeletal muscles for deliberate movement?  
   **Answer:** The somatic nervous system.
2. Which system regulates many involuntary activities such as heart rate and digestion?  
   **Answer:** The autonomic nervous system.
3. Name the two major divisions of the autonomic nervous system.  
   **Answer:** Sympathetic and parasympathetic divisions.

### 7. Human sensory organs and care
**Objective:** Explain the eye, ear, nose, tongue and skin, selected disorders and evidence-based care.  
**Starter:** Match each sensory organ to its receptor type and the stimulus it detects.

1. Which part of the eye controls how much light enters?  
   **Answer:** The iris adjusts pupil size.
2. Which inner-ear structure contains receptors for hearing?  
   **Answer:** The cochlea contains hair cells that detect sound-related vibrations.
3. Which receptors in the nose detect odours?  
   **Answer:** Olfactory receptor neurons in the olfactory epithelium.

### 8. Blind spot, fovea and skin sensitivity
**Objective:** Locate the visual blind spot, explain the fovea and compare skin touch sensitivity.  
**Starter:** Use a printed blind-spot demonstration and a supplied skin-receptor map; learners record observations without touching one another.

1. Why is there a blind spot in each eye?  
   **Answer:** The optic nerve leaves the retina at a region with no photoreceptors.
2. Why does the fovea provide sharp central vision?  
   **Answer:** It has a high density of cones and supports detailed colour vision.
3. Why can fingertips be more sensitive to touch than some other skin regions?  
   **Answer:** They have a high density of touch receptors and small receptive fields.

### 9. Movement structures across animal groups
**Objective:** Compare movement structures in jellyfish, squid, starfish, earthworms, insects, fish and birds.  
**Starter:** Match each animal to its main movement mechanism using labelled diagrams or short videos.

1. How does a jellyfish commonly move through water?  
   **Answer:** By contracting its bell and expelling water for jet propulsion.
2. What structure helps a starfish move?  
   **Answer:** Tube feet operated by the water vascular system.
3. What provides thrust for swimming in many fish?  
   **Answer:** Body and tail-fin movements push water backwards.

### 10. Human bones and muscles
**Objective:** Explain how bones and skeletal muscles work together in movement.  
**Starter:** Use a model of the arm to identify the humerus, radius, ulna, biceps and triceps.

1. What is one function of the skeleton?  
   **Answer:** It supports the body, protects organs and provides attachment points for muscles.
2. Why do skeletal muscles usually work in antagonistic pairs at joints?  
   **Answer:** A muscle can pull but cannot actively push, so an opposing muscle produces movement in the reverse direction.
3. Which muscle contracts to bend the elbow in a typical biceps curl?  
   **Answer:** The biceps brachii contracts while the triceps relaxes.

### 11. Joints and skeletal muscle action
**Objective:** Compare joint types and explain muscle action across joints.  
**Starter:** Examine joint models and classify examples as hinge, ball-and-socket, pivot or gliding joints.

1. What type of joint is the shoulder?  
   **Answer:** A ball-and-socket joint.
2. What type of movement is mainly allowed by the elbow joint?  
   **Answer:** Flexion and extension, like a hinge.
3. What connects skeletal muscle to bone?  
   **Answer:** A tendon.

### 12. Asexual and sexual reproduction in animals
**Objective:** Compare asexual and sexual reproduction in animals.  
**Starter:** Sort examples such as budding, fragmentation and fertilisation into reproductive categories.

1. Does asexual reproduction require the fusion of gametes?  
   **Answer:** No.
2. What is fertilisation?  
   **Answer:** The fusion of male and female gametes to form a zygote.
3. Give one possible advantage of sexual reproduction in changing environments.  
   **Answer:** It can produce genetic variation among offspring.

### 13. Human reproductive system structures
**Objective:** Identify male and female reproductive organs and explain their functions.  
**Starter:** Use medically accurate, age-appropriate diagrams and ask learners to label organs and trace the pathway of gametes.

1. Where are sperm produced?  
   **Answer:** In the seminiferous tubules of the testes.
2. What is the main function of the ovaries?  
   **Answer:** They produce ova and secrete hormones including oestrogen and progesterone.
3. What is the function of the uterus?  
   **Answer:** It is the organ in which an embryo can implant and develop during pregnancy.

### 14. Gamete formation and fertilisation
**Objective:** Describe spermatogenesis, oogenesis and human fertilisation.  
**Starter:** Compare simplified timelines of sperm and egg formation and locate the usual site of fertilisation.

1. What process produces haploid sperm cells?  
   **Answer:** Spermatogenesis, which includes meiosis.
2. What is the usual site of human fertilisation?  
   **Answer:** The ampulla region of the uterine (fallopian) tube.
3. Why is meiosis important in gamete formation?  
   **Answer:** It reduces the chromosome number by half so fertilisation can restore the diploid number.

### 15. Embryonic and post-embryonic development
**Objective:** Compare development in frogs, chickens and humans.  
**Starter:** Place teacher-provided developmental-stage cards in sequence and compare direct development with metamorphosis.

1. What is an embryo?  
   **Answer:** An organism in an early stage of development after fertilisation.
2. What is metamorphosis in frogs?  
   **Answer:** The developmental transformation from a tadpole into an adult frog.
3. Name one extra-embryonic structure that supports a developing chick.  
   **Answer:** The amnion, yolk sac, chorion or allantois; each has a distinct supporting role.

### 16. Hormones and endocrine tissues
**Objective:** Summarise hormone functions and create an endocrine-system concept map.  
**Starter:** Match selected glands or tissues to hormones, target organs and physiological effects.

1. Which gland produces insulin?  
   **Answer:** The pancreas, specifically its beta cells in the islets.
2. What is one major function of thyroid hormones?  
   **Answer:** They help regulate metabolic rate and support growth and development.
3. What is a hormone?  
   **Answer:** A chemical messenger released by cells or glands that affects target cells with appropriate receptors.

### 17. Innate and learned animal behaviour
**Objective:** Compare innate and learned behaviour.  
**Starter:** Sort short animal-behaviour clips into innate, learned or behaviour influenced by both.

1. What is an innate behaviour?  
   **Answer:** A behaviour that is present without the individual having to learn it through experience.
2. What is habituation?  
   **Answer:** A simple form of learning in which response to a repeated, harmless stimulus decreases.
3. Give one example of learned behaviour.  
   **Answer:** A dog learning to respond to a trained signal or a bird learning a song through experience.

### 18. Behaviour and nervous-system evolution
**Objective:** Relate behaviour to the evolution of nervous-system organisation.  
**Starter:** Compare evidence from animal case studies, avoiding the assumption that evolution is a simple ladder of progress.

1. What is one broad trend seen in many animal lineages?  
   **Answer:** Nervous systems can show increasing specialisation and centralisation, although evolution is branching and diverse.
2. How can sensory specialisation influence behaviour?  
   **Answer:** It allows animals to detect particular environmental cues and respond in ways relevant to survival or reproduction.
3. Why should animal nervous systems not be ranked as a simple ladder from inferior to superior?  
   **Answer:** Different nervous systems are adaptations to different environments and ways of life.

### 19. Animal communication
**Objective:** Explain visual, acoustic, chemical and tactile communication between animals.  
**Starter:** Show examples of courtship displays, alarm calls, scent marking and tactile signals; learners identify signal, receiver and likely response.

1. What is a pheromone?  
   **Answer:** A chemical signal released by an organism that affects the behaviour or physiology of another member of the same species.
2. Give one example of acoustic communication.  
   **Answer:** A bird song or a frog call used in territorial or reproductive signalling.
3. Why does the receiver's sensory system matter in animal communication?  
   **Answer:** The receiver must be able to detect and interpret the signal for it to influence behaviour.

- Assess comparisons across animal groups using evidence rather than simplistic evolutionary rankings.
- Use safe, optional sensory demonstrations and accessible alternatives.
- Keep reproductive-health teaching factual, respectful and age appropriate.
