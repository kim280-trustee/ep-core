# M6 Science — Universe, Earth Systems, Climate and Digital Technology

**Source:** OBEC / IPST, *Indicators and Core Learning Content: Must-Know and Should-Know* (2021), printed pp. 158–167  
**Grade:** M6 only  
**Scope:** 25 indicators — W3.1 (10), W3.2 (14), W4.2 (1)  
**Priority recorded:** 24 Must-Know and 1 Should-Know, plus W3.1 M6/4 dual-classified as both Must-Know and Should-Know.

## Lesson 1 — Explain the origin and evolution of the universe

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain changes in the universe's energy, matter, size and temperature after the Big Bang at different stages of cosmic evolution.  
**Starter:** Show a timeline of the universe from the Big Bang to galaxies and stars.

**Formative practice**
1. What theory is widely accepted for the origin of the universe?
   **Answer:** The Big Bang theory.
2. What happened to the universe after the Big Bang?
   **Answer:** It expanded and cooled, allowing particles, atoms, stars and galaxies to form over time.
3. Name two structures that formed during cosmic evolution.
   **Answer:** Galaxies and stars, for example.

## Lesson 2 — Explain evidence supporting the Big Bang theory

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain evidence for the Big Bang using the relationship between galaxy distance and recession speed and the cosmic microwave background.  
**Starter:** Compare a galaxy redshift diagram with an image of the cosmic microwave background.

**Formative practice**
1. What does the relationship between galaxy distance and recession speed show?
   **Answer:** More distant galaxies generally recede faster, supporting an expanding universe.
2. What is the cosmic microwave background?
   **Answer:** Radiation remaining from the early hot universe, observed throughout space.
3. What is its approximate present temperature?
   **Answer:** About 2.73 K.

## Lesson 3 — Describe the Milky Way and the Solar System's location

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain the structure and components of the Milky Way, locate the Solar System within it and relate this to the Milky Way's appearance in the night sky.  
**Starter:** Use a Milky Way diagram and ask students to locate the Solar System.

**Formative practice**
1. What type of galaxy is the Milky Way?
   **Answer:** A barred spiral galaxy.
2. Name three broad structural regions of the Milky Way.
   **Answer:** The central bulge/nucleus, disk and halo.
3. Why does the Milky Way appear as a pale band across a dark sky?
   **Answer:** We view the dense concentration of stars in the galactic disk from within the galaxy.

## Lesson 4 — Explain how stars form from nebulae

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/4 | **Priority:** Must-Know and Should-Know (dual-classified)  
**Learning outcome:** Explain the formation of stars from collapsing nebular material, including changes in pressure, temperature and size before nuclear fusion begins.  
**Starter:** Show a nebula image and a sequence of a collapsing gas cloud forming a star.

**Formative practice**
1. What causes a region of a nebula to collapse?
   **Answer:** Gravity.
2. What happens to pressure and temperature as the collapsing region contracts?
   **Answer:** They increase.
3. What process begins in the core when a star becomes stable and luminous?
   **Answer:** Thermonuclear fusion, initially converting hydrogen into helium.

## Lesson 5 — Explain stellar luminosity and magnitude

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Identify factors affecting stellar luminosity and explain the relationship between luminosity and the magnitude scale used by observers.  
**Starter:** Compare two stars with different apparent brightness and discuss magnitude.

**Formative practice**
1. What is stellar luminosity?
   **Answer:** The total energy a star emits per unit time.
2. What does apparent magnitude describe?
   **Answer:** How bright a star appears to an observer on Earth.
3. Does a smaller magnitude number represent a brighter object?
   **Answer:** Yes. On the magnitude scale, smaller or more negative values indicate greater apparent brightness.

## Lesson 6 — Relate star colour, surface temperature and spectrum

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/6 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how a star's colour relates to its surface temperature and spectrum, and how spectra help classify stars.  
**Starter:** Compare blue, yellow and red star images and infer relative surface temperatures.

**Formative practice**
1. Which stars are generally hotter: blue stars or red stars?
   **Answer:** Blue stars.
2. What does a star's spectrum reveal?
   **Answer:** Information about its light and physical properties, including temperature and composition.
3. Why do astronomers classify stars using spectra?
   **Answer:** Spectral patterns provide evidence for grouping stars by their physical characteristics.

## Lesson 7 — Explain stellar evolution using initial mass

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-07`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/7 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how a star's initial mass influences its evolution, lifetime and final state.  
**Starter:** Give two stars with different initial masses and ask students to predict their life paths.

**Formative practice**
1. Which generally has a shorter lifetime: a high-mass star or a low-mass star?
   **Answer:** A high-mass star, because it uses its energy more rapidly.
2. What mainly determines a star's evolutionary pathway?
   **Answer:** Its initial mass.
3. Why do stars of different masses end their lives differently?
   **Answer:** Their core conditions and fuel use differ, leading to different late stages and remnants.

## Lesson 8 — Explain Solar System formation and habitable planetary conditions

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-08`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/8 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain the formation of the Solar System from the solar nebula, classify broad regions of Solar System bodies and identify conditions that can support life.  
**Starter:** Use a Solar System formation diagram and identify the habitable zone.

**Formative practice**
1. What formed at the centre of the early solar nebula?
   **Answer:** The Sun.
2. Why can liquid water exist on Earth's surface?
   **Answer:** Earth orbits at a distance where temperatures can allow liquid water, with suitable atmospheric conditions.
3. Name one broad region or group of Solar System bodies.
   **Answer:** The inner planets, asteroid belt, outer planets or comets.

## Lesson 9 — Explain the Sun, solar wind and solar storms

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/9 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Describe the Sun's internal and atmospheric structure, explain solar wind and solar storms, and analyse possible effects on Earth, including Thailand.  
**Starter:** Show an aurora or satellite disruption report and trace the connection to solar activity.

**Formative practice**
1. Name the three main internal regions of the Sun.
   **Answer:** The core, radiative zone and convective zone.
2. What is the solar wind?
   **Answer:** A continuous flow of charged particles from the Sun's outer atmosphere into space.
3. How can a strong solar storm affect Earth?
   **Answer:** It can disturb Earth's magnetic environment and disrupt satellites, communications or power systems; it can also produce auroras.

## Lesson 10 — Explain space exploration technologies and applications

**Objective:** `M6-S3_1-10`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.1 ม.6/10 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research space exploration using telescopes across different wavelengths, satellites, spacecraft and space stations, and propose applications of space technology in everyday life or the future.  
**Starter:** Display satellite, telescope and space-station images and match each to its purpose.

**Formative practice**
1. Why do astronomers use telescopes that observe different wavelengths?
   **Answer:** Different wavelengths reveal different types of radiation and information about objects in space.
2. Name one practical use of satellites.
   **Answer:** Communication, navigation, weather forecasting or Earth-resource monitoring.
3. What is a space station used for?
   **Answer:** A crewed orbital laboratory for scientific research and technology testing.

## Lesson 11 — Explain Earth's internal layers and their properties

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain the layers of Earth using chemical composition and mechanical properties, and identify evidence supporting these models.  
**Starter:** Use a cutaway Earth diagram and label compositional and mechanical layers.

**Formative practice**
1. What are Earth's three main compositional layers?
   **Answer:** Crust, mantle and core.
2. What are the five main mechanical layers?
   **Answer:** Lithosphere, asthenosphere, mesosphere, outer core and inner core.
3. What evidence helps scientists infer Earth's internal structure?
   **Answer:** Seismic-wave behaviour, rock and mineral composition and meteorite evidence.

## Lesson 12 — Explain geological evidence for plate tectonics

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain evidence supporting the movement of tectonic plates, including matching continental margins, rock patterns, fossils, palaeomagnetism and ocean-floor features.  
**Starter:** Compare matching coastlines, fossils and seafloor magnetic stripes.

**Formative practice**
1. What is one clue that continents were once joined?
   **Answer:** Matching coastlines, similar rocks or matching fossils on separated continents.
2. What does symmetrical magnetic striping on the ocean floor support?
   **Answer:** Seafloor spreading and movement of tectonic plates.
3. What is the theory that explains large-scale plate movement?
   **Answer:** Plate tectonics.

## Lesson 13 — Identify plate-boundary types and associated evidence

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain causes and types of tectonic plate boundaries and relate them to geological features and hazards.  
**Starter:** Use arrows to model divergent, convergent and transform plate boundaries.

**Formative practice**
1. Name the three main types of plate boundary.
   **Answer:** Divergent, convergent and transform boundaries.
2. What may form where plates converge and one plate subducts?
   **Answer:** A trench and volcanic arc, with earthquakes.
3. What is a common feature of transform boundaries?
   **Answer:** Plates slide past one another along faults, often producing earthquakes.

**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain causes and processes of volcanic eruptions, identify areas at risk and propose monitoring and safety measures.

   **Answer:** Molten rock beneath Earth's surface.
2. Why are many volcanoes located near plate boundaries?
   **Answer:** Plate movement can create pathways and conditions for magma to rise.
3. Name one safety action for people in a volcanic-risk area.
   **Answer:** Follow official evacuation instructions and avoid restricted hazard zones.

## Lesson 15 — Explain earthquakes, impacts and preparedness

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain earthquake causes, magnitude and intensity, impacts and risk areas, and propose monitoring and personal safety measures.  
**Starter:** Show a fault diagram and ask students how stored energy is released.

**Formative practice**
1. What causes most tectonic earthquakes?
   **Answer:** Sudden release of stored energy as rocks slip along a fault.
2. What is the difference between magnitude and intensity?
   **Answer:** Magnitude describes the size of the earthquake source; intensity describes shaking and effects at a location.
3. What should people do during strong indoor shaking?
   **Answer:** Drop, cover and hold on, staying away from windows.

## Lesson 16 — Explain tsunamis and coastal preparedness

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/6 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain tsunami causes, processes and impacts, identify at-risk coastal areas and propose warning and safety measures.  
**Starter:** Compare deep-ocean and coastal tsunami diagrams and identify evacuation routes.

**Formative practice**
1. What can cause a tsunami?
   **Answer:** A large, sudden displacement of seawater, often from an undersea earthquake.
2. Why can a tsunami become much higher near the coast?
   **Answer:** As it enters shallow water, its speed decreases and wave height can increase.
3. What should people do after a natural tsunami warning sign?
   **Answer:** Move immediately to high ground or inland and follow official warnings.

## Lesson 17 — Explain uneven solar energy received across Earth

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-07`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/7 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain why different parts of Earth receive different amounts of solar energy, considering Earth's shape and tilt, surface properties, aerosols and clouds.  
**Starter:** Compare sunlight angles at the equator and poles.

**Formative practice**
1. Why does the equator generally receive more direct sunlight than the poles?
   **Answer:** Earth's curved surface causes sunlight to strike at different angles.
2. How does Earth's axial tilt affect solar energy through the year?
   **Answer:** It changes sun angle and day length between seasons.
3. Name one atmospheric factor that changes incoming solar energy.
   **Answer:** Clouds or aerosols.

## Lesson 18 — Explain atmospheric circulation caused by pressure differences

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-08`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/8 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how horizontal and vertical air movement caused by pressure differences creates atmospheric circulation.  
**Starter:** Use high- and low-pressure symbols to draw horizontal and vertical air movement.

**Formative practice**
1. In which direction does air generally move horizontally?
   **Answer:** From higher pressure toward lower pressure.
2. What tends to happen to air over a low-pressure region?
   **Answer:** It tends to rise.
3. What tends to happen to air over a high-pressure region?
   **Answer:** It tends to sink.

## Lesson 19 — Explain the Coriolis effect on moving air

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/9 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how Earth's rotation causes the Coriolis effect and changes the direction of moving air in the Northern and Southern Hemispheres.  
**Starter:** Use a rotating globe and moving-air arrows to demonstrate Coriolis deflection.

**Formative practice**
1. In which direction is moving air deflected in the Northern Hemisphere?
   **Answer:** To the right of its direction of travel.
2. In which direction is moving air deflected in the Southern Hemisphere?
   **Answer:** To the left.
3. What causes the Coriolis effect?
   **Answer:** Earth's rotation.

**Learning outcome:** Explain latitudinal atmospheric circulation patterns and how they relate to tropical, temperate and polar climate zones.  
**Starter:** Label Hadley, Ferrel and Polar circulation cells on a world map.

**Formative practice**
1. How many major circulation cells are commonly described in each hemisphere?
   **Answer:** Three: Hadley, Ferrel and Polar cells.

   **Answer:** The tropical zone.
3. Why do different latitudes have different climate patterns?
   **Answer:** They receive different solar energy and are affected by global pressure and circulation belts.

**Formative practice**
1. How can a warm ocean current affect nearby coastal climate?
   **Answer:** It can transport heat and make nearby regions milder or warmer.

3. Name one way ocean circulation can affect living organisms.

## Lesson 23 — Explain global climate-change drivers and mitigation

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-13`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/13 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain natural and human factors affecting global climate change and present actions that reduce human contributions.  
**Starter:** Sort climate drivers into natural and human-caused categories.

**Formative practice**
1. Name one human activity that increases greenhouse gases.
   **Answer:** Burning fossil fuels or deforestation.
2. Name one natural factor that can influence climate.
   **Answer:** Changes in solar energy, volcanic aerosols or natural ocean-atmosphere variability.
3. Give one action that can reduce human-caused climate change.
   **Answer:** Improve energy efficiency, use low-carbon energy or protect and restore forests.

## Lesson 24 — Interpret weather maps and use forecasts for planning

**Objective:** `M6-S3_2-14`  
**Official indicator:** ว 3.2 ม.6/14 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Interpret important weather-map symbols and combine weather information from forecasts, radar and satellite images to plan activities safely.  
**Starter:** Give students a simplified weather map and ask them to plan a safe outdoor activity.

**Formative practice**
1. What can a high-pressure symbol on a weather map indicate?
   **Answer:** A region of relatively high atmospheric pressure, often associated with sinking air.
2. Name one source that can supplement a surface weather map.
   **Answer:** Weather radar, satellite images or a forecast application.
3. How can weather information help a farmer?
   **Answer:** It can guide planting, irrigation, harvesting and preparation for severe weather.

**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.6/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Use information technology to present and share information safely and ethically, and analyse how changes in information technology affect life, work, society and culture.  
**Starter:** Compare a public social-media post with a private message and identify safe sharing practices.

**Formative practice**
1. Give one example of sharing information online.
   **Answer:** Publishing a blog post, uploading a video or sharing an infographic.
2. Why should personal information be protected when sharing content?
   **Answer:** To reduce privacy, security and safety risks to oneself and others.
3. Name one way information technology can change work or society.
   **Answer:** It can change communication, job skills, services, culture and how people access information.
