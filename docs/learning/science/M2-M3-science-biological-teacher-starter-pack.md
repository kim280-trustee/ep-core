# M2–M3 Science — Biological Science Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017)  
**Subject:** SCI  
**Coverage:** 17 M2 indicators in ว 1.2 and 11 M3 indicators in ว 1.3. This is a staged biological-science release, not complete M2/M3 Science coverage.

Each lesson maps to one official indicator and includes three practice questions with answer keys. Reproductive-health topics must be taught respectfully and age-appropriately. Do not ask learners to disclose personal experiences or health information; refer medical questions to qualified professionals.


## M2 — Human body systems and reproduction

### Lesson 1 — Identify respiratory organs

**Objective:** `M2-S1_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.2/1  
**Learning outcome:** Identify respiratory organs and describe their functions.

**Teaching activity:** Use labelled diagrams, models, guided data tasks or evidence cards. Ask learners to explain their reasoning and connect the concept to safe, responsible everyday decisions.

**Independent practice**
1. Name the main pathway air follows.  
   **Answer:** Nose or mouth, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli.
2. Where does gas exchange occur?  
   **Answer:** In the alveoli.
3. What carries oxygen in red blood cells?  
   **Answer:** Haemoglobin.

### Lesson 2 — Explain breathing and gas exchange

**Independent practice**
1. What happens to the diaphragm during inhalation?  
   **Answer:** It contracts and moves downward.
2. Which gas moves from alveoli into blood?  
   **Answer:** Oxygen.
3. Which gas moves from blood into alveoli?  
   **Answer:** Carbon dioxide.

## M3 — Genetics and biodiversity

### Lesson 1 — Relate genes, DNA and chromosomes

**Objective:** `M3-S1_3-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.3/1  
**Learning outcome:** Explain relationships among genes, DNA and chromosomes.

**Teaching activity:** Use labelled diagrams, models, guided data tasks or evidence cards. Ask learners to explain their reasoning and connect the concept to safe, responsible everyday decisions.

**Independent practice**
1. What molecule carries hereditary information?  
   **Answer:** DNA.
2. Where are chromosomes found in most human cells?  
   **Answer:** In the nucleus.
3. What is a gene?  
   **Answer:** A section of DNA with information for a functional product or inherited characteristic.

### Lesson 2 — Explain dominant and recessive inheritance

**Objective:** `M3-S1_3-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.3/2  
**Learning outcome:** Explain single-character inheritance with complete dominance.

**Teaching activity:** Use labelled diagrams, models, guided data tasks or evidence cards. Ask learners to explain their reasoning and connect the concept to safe, responsible everyday decisions.

**Independent practice**
1. What is a dominant allele?  
   **Answer:** An allele expressed in the phenotype when at least one copy is present.
2. What is a heterozygous genotype?  
   **Answer:** Two different alleles, such as Tt.
3. If T is dominant, which genotypes show the dominant trait?  
   **Answer:** TT and Tt.

### Lesson 3 — Calculate inheritance ratios

**Objective:** `M3-S1_3-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.3/3  
**Learning outcome:** Determine offspring genotypes and phenotypes for a single-gene cross.

**Teaching activity:** Use labelled diagrams, models, guided data tasks or evidence cards. Ask learners to explain their reasoning and connect the concept to safe, responsible everyday decisions.

**Independent practice**
1. For Tt × Tt, what is the genotype ratio?  
   **Answer:** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt.
2. What is the phenotype ratio under complete dominance?  
   **Answer:** 3 dominant : 1 recessive.
3. Which tool organises allele combinations?  
   **Answer:** A Punnett square.

### Lesson 4 — Compare mitosis and meiosis

   **Answer:** Two.
2. How many cells usually result from meiosis?  
   **Answer:** Four haploid cells.
3. Which process produces gametes?  
   **Answer:** Meiosis.
