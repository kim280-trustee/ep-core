# Upper-Secondary Science — Technology and Computational Thinking

**Source:** OBEC / IPST, *Indicators and Core Learning Content: Must-Know and Should-Know* (2021), pp. 163–167  
**Grade allocation:** M4 (6 indicators), M5 (2 indicators). Each lesson is grade-specific.

## Lesson 1 — Evaluate technology concepts and their wider impacts

**Objective:** `M4-S4_1-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/9 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Analyse the main concepts of a technology, its relationship with other disciplines—especially science and mathematics—and its possible effects on people, society, the economy and the environment.  
**Starter:** Ask students to compare a familiar technology with its effects on users, society and the environment.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a technological system?
   **Answer:** A set of connected parts that work together to achieve a purpose.
2. Name two disciplines that can support technology development.
   **Answer:** Science and mathematics.
3. Why should a technology be assessed for social and environmental impact?
   **Answer:** To identify benefits, risks and unintended consequences before development or use.

## Lesson 2 — Define complex problems and gather relevant information

**Objective:** `M4-S4_1-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/2 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Identify a complex problem or need affecting society, collect and analyse relevant information and ideas, and synthesise possible solution methods while respecting intellectual property.  
**Starter:** Present a local community problem and ask students what evidence they need before designing a solution.

**Formative practice**
1. Give one example of a complex problem affecting society.
   **Answer:** Food waste, transport congestion, energy use or access to healthcare.
2. Why should a design team research a problem before proposing a solution?
   **Answer:** To understand needs, constraints, evidence and existing approaches.
3. What is one intellectual-property issue to consider when using existing designs or information?
   **Answer:** Give appropriate credit and respect licences, copyright and patents.

## Lesson 3 — Design and communicate a solution under constraints

**Objective:** `M4-S4_1-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/3 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Design a solution by comparing necessary information and available resources, then communicate the approach using suitable drawings, diagrams, flowcharts or software and plan the work.  
**Starter:** Give teams a design brief with a limited budget, time and materials.

**Formative practice**
1. Name two constraints that may affect a design solution.
   **Answer:** Budget, time, materials, tools or available information.
2. Give one way to communicate a design idea.
   **Answer:** A sketch, diagram, flowchart, model or digital presentation.
3. Why is a work plan useful?
   **Answer:** It sets tasks and timing, helping the team meet its goal and reduce errors.

## Lesson 4 — Test, evaluate and improve a designed solution

**Objective:** `M4-S4_1-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/4 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Test and evaluate a product or process against requirements, analyse defects, improve the solution and communicate results and possible further development.  
**Starter:** Show a simple prototype with a visible defect and ask students how to test and improve it.

**Formative practice**
1. What should a test compare a product against?
   **Answer:** Its objectives, requirements and constraints.
2. Why might a team repeat a test after making an improvement?
   **Answer:** To check whether the change fixed the problem and did not create another defect.
3. Name one way to present a design project result.
   **Answer:** A demonstration, report, poster, exhibition or online presentation.

## Lesson 5 — Select materials, mechanisms and tools safely

**Objective:** `M4-S4_1-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/5 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Apply knowledge of materials, tools, mechanisms, electrical and electronic components, and more complex technologies to solve problems or develop work appropriately and safely.  
**Starter:** Display common materials and tools and ask students to choose safe options for a simple build.

**Formative practice**
1. Why should a material be selected for its properties?
   **Answer:** Different materials have different strength, flexibility, conductivity and durability.
2. Name one simple electronic component that may be used in a project.
   **Answer:** An LDR sensor, resistor, switch or circuit module.
3. Why is safe tool use important?
   **Answer:** It reduces the risk of injury and damage and helps protect equipment.

## Lesson 6 — Develop interdisciplinary projects using computational thinking

**Objective:** `M4-S4_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.4/1 | **Grade:** M4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Apply computational-thinking concepts to develop a creative project that integrates other subjects and connects with real-life situations.  
**Starter:** Ask students to plan a real-life project using decomposition and a simple algorithm.

**Formative practice**
1. What is computational thinking?
   **Answer:** A structured way of solving problems using decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction and algorithms.
2. Give one real-life project that could use computational thinking.
   **Answer:** A plant-watering system, energy monitor or transport-route planner.
3. Why should a project connect to a real-life need?
   **Answer:** It makes the solution useful and gives a clear purpose for testing.

## Lesson 7 — Apply interdisciplinary knowledge in a project

**Objective:** `M5-S4_1-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.5/1 | **Grade:** M5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Apply knowledge, skills and available resources from different disciplines to plan and carry out a project that solves a problem or improves a product or process.  
**Starter:** Give groups a project brief that requires science, mathematics and technology skills.

**Formative practice**
1. What is an interdisciplinary project?
   **Answer:** A project that combines knowledge or skills from more than one subject area.
2. Name two resources a project team should plan for.
   **Answer:** Time, budget, materials, tools, information or people.
3. What should happen after a prototype is built?
   **Answer:** Test it, evaluate results, improve it and present the solution.

## Lesson 8 — Use data and computing to improve real products or services

**Objective:** `M5-S4_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.5/1 | **Grade:** M5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Collect and analyse data and use computer science, digital media and information technology creatively to solve real-life problems or add value to services and products.  
**Starter:** Ask students to plan a real-life project using decomposition and a simple algorithm.

**Formative practice**
1. Why should data be checked and prepared before analysis?
   **Answer:** To improve accuracy, consistency and readiness for processing.
2. Name one way to communicate an analysed dataset visually.
   **Answer:** A bar chart, scatter plot or histogram.
3. Give one real-life service that could be improved using data.
   **Answer:** Food planning, bus-stop placement, packaging design or a local service recommendation.

**Teacher note:** Emphasise evidence, constraints, iteration, intellectual-property respect and safe tool use. These are core curriculum indicators, not optional enrichment activities.
