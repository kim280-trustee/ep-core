# M4 Physics — Foundations, Measurement, Motion and Forces

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017)  
**Subject / grade:** Physics, M4  
**Official coverage:** Additional Physics learning outcomes 1–7  
**Coverage:** 7 lesson starters; 21 practice questions with answer keys  
**Use:** Teacher planning resource. Prefer diagrams, simulations and low-risk demonstrations; do not use hazardous equipment or unsafe suspended loads.

## Lesson 1 — The nature and development of physics

**Objective:** M4-PHY-FOUNDATIONS-01 — Explain how physics knowledge is developed and how physical ideas contribute to new knowledge and technology.

**Starter:** Show a familiar technology such as a mobile phone, bicycle brake or solar panel. Ask learners which physics ideas help it work. Build a simple sequence: observation → question → investigation/model → evidence → explanation → further questions.

**Practice**
1. Name two ways scientists develop knowledge in physics.
   **Answer:** Observation and experimentation; modelling and analysing evidence are also valid.
2. Why do scientific explanations change when new evidence becomes available?
   **Answer:** Scientific explanations are tested against evidence and may be refined when better evidence or models become available.
3. Give one example of physics knowledge being used in technology.
   **Answer:** Any valid example, such as electricity in phones, optics in cameras, or mechanics in vehicle safety.

## Lesson 2 — Measurement, uncertainty and graphs

**Objective:** M4-PHY-MEASUREMENT-02 — Measure and report physical quantities appropriately, consider uncertainty, and analyse straight-line graphs.

**Starter:** Compare measuring a desk with a ruler and estimating its length by eye. Discuss why measurements include uncertainty. Demonstrate SI units, sensible significant figures and plotting a straight-line graph from supplied data.

**Practice**
1. Convert 250 cm to metres.
   **Answer:** 2.50 m.
2. A measured length is 12.4 cm with an estimated uncertainty of ±0.1 cm. State the result.
   **Answer:** (12.4 ± 0.1) cm.
3. A straight-line distance–time graph rises from 0 m at 0 s to 20 m at 5 s. Find its gradient and state what it represents.
   **Answer:** Gradient = 20/5 = 4 m/s; it represents velocity for this straight-line graph.

## Lesson 3 — Uniformly accelerated straight-line motion

**Objective:** M4-PHY-MOTION-03 — Use graphs and equations to explain uniformly accelerated straight-line motion and investigate gravitational acceleration.

**Starter:** Use a trolley video or simulation. Ask learners how velocity changes over equal time intervals. Connect the slope of a velocity–time graph to acceleration and its area to displacement.

**Practice**
1. A cyclist increases velocity from 2 m/s to 10 m/s in 4 s. Calculate acceleration.
   **Answer:** a = (10 − 2)/4 = 2 m/s².
2. An object starts from rest and accelerates at 3 m/s² for 5 s. Find its final velocity.
   **Answer:** v = u + at = 0 + 3 × 5 = 15 m/s.
3. What is the approximate gravitational acceleration near Earth's surface?
   **Answer:** 9.8 m/s² downward (often approximated as 10 m/s²).

## Lesson 4 — Forces and resultant force

**Objective:** M4-PHY-FORCES-04 — Explain how forces affect motion and determine the resultant of two forces acting at an angle.

**Starter:** Show two learners pulling a light object in different directions using a diagram or simulation. Ask whether the effects simply add. Introduce force as a vector with magnitude and direction.

**Practice**
1. Two forces of 5 N and 3 N act in the same direction. Find the resultant.
   **Answer:** 8 N in that direction.
2. Two forces of 8 N act in opposite directions. Find the resultant.
   **Answer:** 0 N; the forces balance.
3. Two perpendicular forces of 3 N and 4 N act on an object. Find the magnitude of the resultant.
   **Answer:** R = √(3² + 4²) = 5 N.

## Lesson 5 — Newton laws and free-body diagrams

**Objective:** M4-PHY-NEWTON-05 — Draw free-body diagrams and apply Newton laws to motion.

**Starter:** Ask why a passenger moves forward when a moving bus stops. Use simple diagrams to identify weight, normal force, friction and applied force. Emphasise that each force acts on the selected object.

**Practice**
1. State Newton's first law.
   **Answer:** An object remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless acted on by a non-zero resultant external force.
2. A 4 kg object experiences a resultant force of 12 N. Calculate acceleration.
   **Answer:** a = F/m = 12/4 = 3 m/s².
3. A book rests on a horizontal table. Name the two main vertical forces acting on the book.
   **Answer:** Weight downward and normal contact force upward; they balance when the book is at rest.

## Lesson 6 — Universal gravitation and gravitational fields

**Objective:** M4-PHY-GRAVITATION-06 — Explain universal gravitation, gravitational fields and weight.

**Starter:** Compare the mass of an object on Earth and on the Moon. Ask what changes and what remains the same. Distinguish mass from weight and introduce gravitational field strength.

**Practice**
1. What is the difference between mass and weight?
   **Answer:** Mass measures inertia and is measured in kilograms; weight is gravitational force and is measured in newtons.
2. Calculate the weight of a 5 kg object where g = 10 N/kg.
   **Answer:** W = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N.
3. If the distance between two point masses increases, what happens to the gravitational force between them?
   **Answer:** It decreases according to the inverse-square relationship.

## Lesson 7 — Friction

**Objective:** M4-PHY-FRICTION-07 — Explain static and kinetic friction, determine friction coefficients and apply the ideas to everyday situations.

**Starter:** Compare a shoe on a dry floor and a smooth wet surface. Ask why friction matters for walking and braking. Use diagrams or supplied data to distinguish static friction from kinetic friction.

**Practice**
1. What is the difference between static friction and kinetic friction?
   **Answer:** Static friction acts when surfaces do not slide relative to each other; kinetic friction acts while surfaces slide.
2. A 10 N friction force acts when the normal force is 25 N. Calculate the coefficient of kinetic friction.
   **Answer:** μ = F/N = 10/25 = 0.40.
3. Give one useful and one harmful effect of friction.
   **Answer:** Useful: walking or braking. Harmful: wear or unwanted heating of machine parts.

## Teaching and assessment notes

- Keep vector direction and sign conventions explicit in motion and force calculations.
- Use appropriate SI units and sensible significant figures; discuss uncertainty rather than presenting measurements as exact.
- Distinguish mass (kg), weight (N), and gravitational field strength (N/kg).
- A free-body diagram includes forces acting on one selected object, not forces that object exerts on other objects.
- The coefficient of friction is dimensionless. Use safe surfaces and teacher-approved data; do not ask students to create dangerous slip conditions.
- Use simulations and supplied data for investigations that require specialist equipment.
