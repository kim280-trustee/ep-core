# M5 Chemistry — Gas Laws and Properties Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017)  
**Subject / grade:** Chemistry, M5  
**Official strand:** Additional Chemistry strand 1  
**Coverage:** 6 official M5 learning outcomes; 6 lesson starters; 18 practice questions with answer keys  
**Use:** Teacher planning resource. Use simulations, worked datasets and teacher-controlled demonstrations. Do not use sealed gas-generating reactions or pressurised equipment without approved risk controls.

## Lesson 1 — Boyle, Charles and Gay-Lussac laws
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-01 — Explain and calculate relationships among gas volume, pressure and absolute temperature when the other relevant variable is held constant.
**Starter / learning task:** What happens to a gas when its container is compressed or heated? Compare particle diagrams and simple datasets. Emphasise kelvin for gas-law calculations.

**Practice**
1. State Boyle's law.
   **Answer:** For a fixed amount of gas at constant temperature, pressure is inversely proportional to volume.
2. What temperature scale is used in gas-law calculations?
   **Answer:** Kelvin.
3. At constant pressure, what happens to gas volume when its absolute temperature doubles?
   **Answer:** The volume doubles, provided the amount of gas remains constant.

## Lesson 2 — The combined gas law
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-02 — Calculate a gas variable when pressure, volume and temperature change between two states for a fixed amount of gas.
**Starter / learning task:** How can the initial and final states of a gas be compared? Use a two-state table and the combined gas law; require students to show unit conversions.

**Practice**
1. Write the combined gas law.
   **Answer:** P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ for a fixed amount of gas.
2. A gas has a volume of 2.0 L at 100 kPa. At constant temperature, what is its volume at 200 kPa?
   **Answer:** 1.0 L, because pressure doubles while volume halves.
3. Why must temperature be converted to kelvin?
   **Answer:** Gas-law proportionalities use absolute temperature, whose zero corresponds to zero thermal energy in the idealised model.

## Lesson 3 — Avogadro and ideal gas laws
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-03 — Calculate gas volume, pressure, temperature, amount or mass using Avogadro relationships and the ideal gas equation.
**Starter / learning task:** How are gas amount and volume related at fixed pressure and temperature? Move from particle-count models to PV = nRT calculations.

**Practice**
1. State Avogadro's law.
   **Answer:** At constant temperature and pressure, equal volumes of gases contain equal numbers of particles; volume is proportional to amount in moles.
2. Write the ideal gas equation.
   **Answer:** PV = nRT.
3. What must be checked before substituting values into PV = nRT?
   **Answer:** Use a consistent gas constant and units for pressure, volume and temperature.

## Lesson 4 — Dalton's law of partial pressures
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-04 — Use Dalton's partial-pressure relationships to calculate component partial pressures, mole fractions or amounts in a gas mixture.
**Starter / learning task:** How does each gas contribute to the total pressure of a mixture? Use a labelled particle model and sample partial-pressure data.

**Practice**
1. State Dalton's law.
   **Answer:** The total pressure of a mixture of non-reacting gases equals the sum of their partial pressures.
2. A mixture contains gases with partial pressures of 30 kPa and 70 kPa. What is the total pressure?
   **Answer:** 100 kPa.
3. What is a mole fraction?
   **Answer:** The moles of one component divided by the total moles in the mixture.

## Lesson 5 — Kinetic molecular theory and Graham's law
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-05 — Use kinetic molecular theory to explain gas diffusion and calculate or compare diffusion rates using molar masses.
**Starter / learning task:** Why do lighter gas particles generally diffuse faster than heavier ones at the same temperature? Use particle-motion diagrams and a comparison dataset.

**Practice**
1. What causes gas pressure in the kinetic molecular model?
   **Answer:** Collisions of gas particles with the walls of their container.
2. State Graham's law for two gases.
   **Answer:** At the same conditions, the ratio of diffusion rates is the square root of the inverse ratio of their molar masses.
3. Which diffuses faster at the same temperature, hydrogen or oxygen?
   **Answer:** Hydrogen, because its molar mass is lower.

## Lesson 6 — Gas laws in daily life and industry
**Objective:** M5-CHEM-GAS-06 — Research and explain applications of gas properties and gas laws in everyday phenomena and industry.
**Starter / learning task:** Where do gas laws help explain weather, breathing, tyres, balloons or industrial gas storage? Ask learners to connect an example to a law and identify safety limits.

**Practice**
1. Why does a flexible balloon shrink when cooled?
   **Answer:** At approximately constant external pressure, the gas volume decreases as its absolute temperature decreases.
2. Why must a gas cylinder be protected from heat?
   **Answer:** Heating a fixed-volume cylinder raises gas pressure and can create a serious hazard.
3. Why are real gases not perfectly described by PV = nRT under all conditions?
   **Answer:** Real particles have finite volume and intermolecular attractions, especially at high pressure and low temperature.
