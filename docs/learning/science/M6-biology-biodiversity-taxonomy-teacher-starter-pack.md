# M6 Biology — Biodiversity, Origin of Life and Classification Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education Core Curriculum B.E. 2551 (2008), revised B.E. 2560 (2017)  
**Subject / grade:** Biology, M6  
**Official strand:** Additional Biology strand 2  
**Coverage:** 5 official M6 learning outcomes; 5 lesson starters; 15 practice questions with answer keys  
**Use:** Teacher planning resource. Use diagrams, fossil and molecular datasets, safe local observations and fictional classification sets. Present origin-of-life explanations as scientific hypotheses evaluated against evidence, not as settled details.

## Lesson 1 — Levels and importance of biodiversity
**Objective:** M6-BIO-BIODIV-01 — Explain genetic, species and ecosystem diversity and how they are connected.
**Starter / learning task:** Explain genetic, species and ecosystem diversity and how they are connected. Have learners justify their explanations with examples, diagrams or evidence from supplied resources.

**Practice**
1. What is genetic diversity?
   **Answer:** Variation in genetic information among individuals or populations of the same species.
2. How is species diversity different from ecosystem diversity?
   **Answer:** Species diversity concerns the variety and relative abundance of species; ecosystem diversity concerns the variety of habitats and ecological systems.
3. Why does biodiversity matter to people and ecosystems?
   **Answer:** It supports ecosystem functions and services, resilience, food resources, medicines and cultural values.

## Lesson 2 — Origin of early cells and unicellular life
**Objective:** M6-BIO-BIODIV-02 — Evaluate scientific hypotheses and evidence about early cells and the evolution of unicellular organisms.
**Starter / learning task:** Evaluate scientific hypotheses and evidence about early cells and the evolution of unicellular organisms. Have learners justify their explanations with examples, diagrams or evidence from supplied resources.

**Practice**
1. What is a protocell in origin-of-life research?
   **Answer:** A proposed early cell-like system with a boundary and some life-like chemical processes; it is not itself a confirmed living ancestor.
2. What evidence supports the ancient existence of microbial life?
   **Answer:** Evidence includes ancient microbial structures, chemical signatures and geological records, interpreted with uncertainty.
3. Why should explanations of life's origin distinguish evidence from hypotheses?
   **Answer:** The early Earth record is incomplete, so scientific models are evaluated and revised as new evidence appears.

## Lesson 3 — Major groups of organisms
**Objective:** M6-BIO-BIODIV-03 — Compare bacteria, protists, plants, fungi and animals using cellular and nutritional characteristics.
**Starter / learning task:** Compare bacteria, protists, plants, fungi and animals using cellular and nutritional characteristics. Have learners justify their explanations with examples, diagrams or evidence from supplied resources.

**Practice**
1. Are bacteria prokaryotic or eukaryotic?
   **Answer:** Prokaryotic; they lack a membrane-bound nucleus.
2. What feature distinguishes fungi from plants in nutrition?
   **Answer:** Fungi absorb nutrients from organic material; plants generally photosynthesise, although there are exceptions.
3. Why is appearance alone unreliable for classification?
   **Answer:** Similar forms can evolve independently; cellular, reproductive and molecular evidence provides stronger relationships.

## Lesson 4 — Taxonomic hierarchy and scientific names
**Objective:** M6-BIO-BIODIV-04 — Classify organisms through taxonomic ranks and write species-level scientific names correctly.
**Starter / learning task:** Classify organisms through taxonomic ranks and write species-level scientific names correctly. Have learners justify their explanations with examples, diagrams or evidence from supplied resources.

**Practice**
1. Name the common taxonomic ranks from broad to specific.
   **Answer:** Domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species.
2. How is a binomial scientific name written?
   **Answer:** The genus begins with a capital letter, the specific epithet is lowercase, and the whole name is italicised when typed.
3. Why are scientific names useful?
   **Answer:** They provide a standard international name that reduces ambiguity among common names.

## Lesson 5 — Dichotomous keys
**Objective:** M6-BIO-BIODIV-05 — Construct and apply a dichotomous key using observable paired characteristics.
**Starter / learning task:** Construct and apply a dichotomous key using observable paired characteristics. Have learners justify their explanations with examples, diagrams or evidence from supplied resources.

**Practice**
1. What is a dichotomous key?
   **Answer:** An identification tool that uses a sequence of paired choices to identify an organism or specimen.
2. What makes a useful pair of statements in a key?
   **Answer:** The two alternatives are clear, mutually exclusive and based on observable characteristics.
3. Why should a key avoid vague terms such as 'large' without a reference?
   **Answer:** Different users may interpret them differently, reducing reliable identification.
