# M4 Science — Ecology and Environmental Change Teacher Starter Pack

**Source:** OBEC / IPST, *Indicators and Core Learning Content: Must-Know and Should-Know* (2021), pp. 143–144  
**Grade:** M4 only | **Scope:** ว 1.1 ม.4/1–4 (2 Must-Know, 2 Should-Know)

## Lesson 1 — Geography and biomes

**Objective:** M4-S1_1-01  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.1 ม.4/1 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Investigate how geographic conditions, climate and rainfall relate to biome diversity, and give examples of different biomes.  
**Starter:** Show rainforest, desert, grassland and tundra photographs. Students identify differences and suggest how temperature and rainfall shape each environment.

1. What is a biome?  
   **Answer:** A large ecological region characterised by its climate and typical communities of plants and animals.
2. Name two factors that help determine a biome.  
   **Answer:** Temperature/climate and rainfall.
3. Why can regions at similar latitudes have different ecosystems?  
   **Answer:** They may differ in altitude, rainfall, temperature, soils or other geographic conditions.

## Lesson 2 — Ecological succession

**Objective:** M4-S1_1-02  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.1 ม.4/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Investigate causes of ecological succession and give examples of community change over time.  
**Starter:** Present bare rock, pioneer plants, grasses, shrubs and trees. Students arrange the stages and explain the changes.

1. What is ecological succession?  
   **Answer:** The gradual change in the species and structure of a biological community over time.
2. Give one natural event that can begin succession.  
   **Answer:** A volcanic eruption, retreating glacier, landslide or abandoned field.
3. What is a pioneer species?  
   **Answer:** An early-arriving organism that can establish in a newly exposed or disturbed area.

## Lesson 3 — Population size and ecosystem factors

**Objective:** M4-S1_1-03  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.1 ม.4/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how physical and biological changes affect population size.  
**Starter:** Show a rabbit population graph before and after a drought. Students identify the change and propose causes.

1. Name one physical factor that can change population size.  
   **Answer:** Temperature, rainfall, water availability, light or habitat conditions.
2. Name one biological factor that can change population size.  
   **Answer:** Food availability, competition, predation, disease or parasites.
3. How might a long drought affect a plant-eating animal population?  
   **Answer:** Reduced food and water can lower survival and reproduction, causing the population to decrease.

## Lesson 4 — Environmental impacts and conservation

**Objective:** M4-S1_1-04  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.1 ม.4/4 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Investigate environmental problems and present conservation responses.  
**Starter:** Use a local case study such as polluted waterways, forest loss or plastic waste. Groups identify causes, impacts and realistic responses.

1. Give one human activity that can damage natural resources.  
   **Answer:** Deforestation, overfishing, unsafe mining or releasing untreated waste.
2. State one possible effect of forest loss.  
   **Answer:** Habitat destruction, soil erosion, reduced biodiversity or increased runoff.
3. Suggest one practical conservation action for a school or community.  
   **Answer:** Restore native trees, reduce waste, protect waterways, conserve water or monitor local habitats.

**Teacher note:** Use local examples and evidence-based discussion. Succession pathways depend on local conditions and disturbance history.
