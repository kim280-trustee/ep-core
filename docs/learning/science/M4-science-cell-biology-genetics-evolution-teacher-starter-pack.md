## Lesson 5 — Explain cell membrane structure and transport

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how the lipid bilayer and membrane proteins control movement of substances, comparing diffusion, facilitated diffusion, active transport, endocytosis and exocytosis.  
**Starter:** Use a simple membrane diagram. Students sort examples of substances by how they cross the membrane.

**Formative practice**
1. What are the two main components of the cell membrane?
   **Answer:** A phospholipid bilayer and embedded proteins.
2. How is facilitated diffusion different from active transport?
   **Answer:** Facilitated diffusion moves substances down a concentration gradient without energy; active transport uses energy to move substances against a gradient.
3. How can a cell take in a large particle?
   **Answer:** By endocytosis, which encloses the material in a membrane vesicle.

## Lesson 6 — Explain kidney regulation of water and blood solutes

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how the kidneys remove nitrogenous wastes and regulate excess water and dissolved substances in the blood.  
**Starter:** Show a simple kidney-to-urine flow diagram. Students identify what the kidneys remove from blood.

**Formative practice**
1. What is one important function of the kidneys?
   **Answer:** They remove nitrogen-containing wastes from the blood.
2. Why does the body need to regulate water in the blood?
   **Answer:** To maintain a stable internal environment for cells and body processes.
3. What may happen to urine output when a person is dehydrated?
   **Answer:** The kidneys conserve more water, so urine volume usually decreases and becomes more concentrated.

## Lesson 7 — Explain blood acid-base balance

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how the kidneys regulate hydrogen and bicarbonate ions and how the lungs remove carbon dioxide to help maintain blood pH.  
**Starter:** Compare breathing rate and kidney function in a simple balance diagram.

**Formative practice**
1. Which two organ systems help regulate blood acid-base balance?
   **Answer:** The kidneys and lungs.
2. What gas do the lungs remove that affects blood pH?
   **Answer:** Carbon dioxide.
3. How do the kidneys help control blood pH?
   **Answer:** They adjust the excretion or reabsorption of hydrogen and bicarbonate ions.

## Lesson 8 — Explain body temperature regulation

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how blood circulation, skin and skeletal muscles help regulate internal body temperature.  
**Starter:** Ask students why skin may become red and sweaty during exercise.

**Formative practice**
1. What do sweat glands release to help cool the body?
   **Answer:** Sweat.
2. How does increased blood flow to the skin help cool the body?
   **Answer:** More heat can be transferred from the blood to the skin and surroundings.
3. Why do skeletal muscles shiver when a person is cold?
   **Answer:** Muscle contractions produce heat.

## Lesson 9 — Compare non-specific and specific immune responses

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain and diagram non-specific and specific body responses to foreign substances, including phagocytes, B lymphocytes, T lymphocytes and antibodies.  
**Starter:** Give groups cards labelled skin barrier, phagocyte, B cell, T cell and antibody. Students place them in a defence sequence.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a non-specific immune response?
   **Answer:** A general defence that acts against many types of pathogens, such as phagocytosis.
2. Which lymphocytes can produce antibodies?
   **Answer:** B lymphocytes.
3. What is one role of T lymphocytes?
   **Answer:** They coordinate immune responses or destroy infected and abnormal cells.

## Lesson 10 — Investigate disorders of the immune system

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/6 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research and explain examples of illnesses or symptoms caused by abnormal immune-system responses, including excessive responses or reactions against the body's own antigens.  
**Starter:** Students match allergy and autoimmune examples to the type of immune-system problem.

**Formative practice**
1. What can happen when the immune system reacts too strongly to a harmless antigen?
   **Answer:** An allergic or hypersensitivity reaction may occur.
2. What is an autoimmune condition?
   **Answer:** A condition in which the immune system reacts against the body's own cells or tissues.
3. Give one example of an immune-system disorder.
   **Answer:** An allergy or an autoimmune disease such as type 1 diabetes.

## Lesson 11 — Explain HIV-related immune deficiency

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-07`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/7 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how HIV infection can damage T cells and weaken immune defence, using accurate and non-stigmatising scientific language.  
**Starter:** Use a labelled immune-system diagram to show the role of helper T cells.

**Formative practice**
1. Which immune cells are especially affected by HIV?
   **Answer:** CD4 T-helper cells.
2. How can loss of helper T cells affect the body?
   **Answer:** It weakens coordination of immune responses and increases vulnerability to certain infections.
3. Can HIV be transmitted by sharing food or classroom equipment?
   **Answer:** No. HIV is not transmitted through ordinary social contact such as sharing food or classroom equipment.

## Lesson 12 — Test nutrients synthesised by plants

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-08`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/8 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Carry out appropriate food tests and identify nutrient types that plants synthesise, including carbohydrates, proteins and lipids.  
**Starter:** Demonstrate or discuss iodine, Benedict's and Biuret tests with safe prepared samples.

**Formative practice**
1. Which reagent is commonly used to test for starch?
   **Answer:** Iodine solution; a positive result turns blue-black.
2. Which test can detect reducing sugars?
   **Answer:** Benedict's solution with heating; a positive result changes from blue toward green, yellow, orange or brick-red.
3. What colour change indicates a positive Biuret test for protein?
   **Answer:** Blue changes to lilac or purple.

## Lesson 13 — Investigate useful substances produced by plants

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/9 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research and discuss examples of substances produced by plants and their uses in medicine, pest control, antimicrobial products and industry.  
**Starter:** Display familiar plant-derived products such as herbal products, natural fibres or plant oils. Students identify source and use.

**Formative practice**
1. Give one example of a useful substance produced by a plant.
   **Answer:** Aloe compounds, quinine, caffeine, essential oils or natural rubber.
2. How can some plant compounds be useful in agriculture?
   **Answer:** They may help repel insects or control pests, when used safely and appropriately.
3. Why should plant-derived products be tested before medical use?
   **Answer:** Their safety, dose, effectiveness and possible side effects need scientific evaluation.

## Lesson 14 — Investigate external factors affecting plant growth

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-10`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/10 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Design and conduct a fair experiment to explain how external factors such as light, water, mineral nutrients, carbon dioxide and oxygen affect plant growth.  
**Starter:** Students plan a seedling experiment changing only one factor while keeping other conditions constant.

**Formative practice**
1. Name two external factors that can affect plant growth.
   **Answer:** Light, water, mineral nutrients, carbon dioxide or oxygen.
2. Why should only one factor be changed in a fair test?
   **Answer:** To identify which factor caused the observed difference.
3. What measurements could students use to compare plant growth?
   **Answer:** Plant height, leaf number, leaf area or biomass.

## Lesson 15 — Explain synthetic plant growth regulators

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-11`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/11 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research synthetic substances modelled on plant hormones and give examples of how they are used to control plant growth or improve agricultural production.  
**Starter:** Show examples of fruit ripening, rooting or weed control and ask which growth response is being managed.

**Formative practice**
1. What are plant growth regulators?
   **Answer:** Substances that influence plant growth and development, including natural hormones and synthetic compounds.
2. Why might farmers use a synthetic growth regulator?
   **Answer:** To influence rooting, flowering, fruit development, ripening or other growth processes.
3. Why must growth regulators be used according to guidance?
   **Answer:** Incorrect use can harm crops, people or the environment and may leave residues.

## Lesson 16 — Explain plant responses to stimuli

**Objective:** `M4-S1_2-12`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.2 ม.4/12 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain plant responses to stimuli, including directional tropisms and non-directional movements that affect survival.  
**Starter:** Show a time-lapse of a shoot bending toward light and a sensitive plant folding its leaves.

**Formative practice**
1. What is phototropism?
   **Answer:** Directional plant growth in response to light.
2. How does a root commonly respond to gravity?
   **Answer:** It grows in the direction of gravity (positive gravitropism).
3. Give one example of a non-directional plant movement.
   **Answer:** The folding of sensitive-plant leaves or opening and closing of some flowers.

## Lesson 17 — Explain the relationship between genes, proteins and traits

**Objective:** `M4-S1_3-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how a gene is a DNA sequence that provides information for protein synthesis and how proteins contribute to inherited characteristics.  
**Starter:** Use a simple DNA → gene expression → protein → trait flow diagram.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a gene?
   **Answer:** A segment of DNA containing information used to produce a functional product, often a protein.
2. How can a gene affect a characteristic?
   **Answer:** Its information can determine a protein that influences how a trait develops.
3. What are the building blocks of DNA?
   **Answer:** Nucleotides.

## Lesson 18 — Explain sex-linked and multiple-allele inheritance

**Objective:** `M4-S1_3-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain inheritance patterns controlled by genes on sex chromosomes and by multiple alleles, using examples such as colour blindness, haemophilia and ABO blood groups.  
**Starter:** Students complete a simple ABO inheritance example and identify a sex-linked trait example.

**Formative practice**
1. What does sex-linked inheritance mean?
   **Answer:** A trait is controlled by a gene located on a sex chromosome.
2. Give one example of a sex-linked condition used in genetics lessons.
   **Answer:** Red-green colour blindness or haemophilia.
3. How many common alleles are involved in the ABO blood group system?
   **Answer:** Three: Iᴬ, Iᴮ and i.

## Lesson 19 — Explain effects of DNA sequence changes

**Objective:** `M4-S1_3-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how changes in DNA nucleotide sequences, chromosome structure or chromosome number may change an organism's characteristics, with effects that can be harmful, beneficial or neutral.  
**Starter:** Compare a normal and altered DNA sequence and discuss how a protein may be affected.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a mutation?
   **Answer:** A change in genetic material, such as a DNA sequence or chromosome change.
2. Can every mutation cause a visible change?
   **Answer:** No. Some mutations have no noticeable effect; others may be harmful or beneficial.
3. How might a DNA sequence change affect a trait?
   **Answer:** It may alter a protein or its production, which can change the characteristic.

## Lesson 20 — Give examples of useful applications of mutation

**Objective:** `M4-S1_3-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/4 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research and give examples of how induced mutations, including those caused by radiation or chemicals, can be used to develop organisms with useful characteristics.  
**Starter:** Students compare examples of crop varieties developed through mutation breeding.

**Formative practice**
1. What is mutation breeding?
   **Answer:** A method that uses induced genetic changes to develop organisms with selected useful traits.
2. Name one method used to induce mutations in breeding programmes.
   **Answer:** Radiation or certain chemicals, under controlled conditions.
3. Why must new varieties be tested after mutation breeding?
   **Answer:** To check useful traits, stability, safety and performance.

**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research and discuss applications of DNA technology in medicine, agriculture and forensic science, considering biosafety, bioethics and social impacts.  
**Starter:** Use a case study such as DNA fingerprinting or a genetically modified crop. Students list benefits and concerns.

**Formative practice**
1. Give one medical application of DNA technology.
   **Answer:** Producing medicines or vaccines using genetically engineered organisms.
2. How can DNA fingerprinting be used?
   **Answer:** To compare biological samples for identity or biological relationships.
3. Name two issues that should be considered when using DNA technology.
   **Answer:** Biosafety and bioethics; social or environmental impacts should also be considered.

## Lesson 22 — Explain biodiversity as a result of evolution

**Objective:** `M4-S1_3-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 1.3 ม.4/6 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain and give examples of biodiversity arising from genetic variation, mutation and natural selection over generations.  
**Starter:** Compare adaptations of organisms in different environments and ask how inherited variation can affect survival.

**Formative practice**
1. What is biodiversity?
   **Answer:** The variety of living organisms, including diversity within species, between species and of ecosystems.
2. What creates new genetic variation?
   **Answer:** Mutation and processes such as recombination during sexual reproduction.
3. How can natural selection change a population over generations?
   **Answer:** Individuals with inherited traits that improve reproductive success may leave more offspring, making those traits more common.

**Teacher note:** Keep practical work age-appropriate and follow laboratory safety guidance. Use respectful, scientifically accurate language when teaching HIV and immune-system topics.
