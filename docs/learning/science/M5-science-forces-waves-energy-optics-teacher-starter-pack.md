## Lesson 1 — Interpret velocity-time data and explain acceleration

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Analyse velocity-time data to explain acceleration as the rate of change of velocity, including its direction relative to velocity.  
**Starter:** Show a velocity-time graph and ask students to identify intervals of speeding up, slowing down and constant velocity.

**Formative practice**
1. What is acceleration?
   **Answer:** The rate at which velocity changes with time.
2. What does a horizontal line on a velocity-time graph show?
   **Answer:** Constant velocity and zero acceleration.
3. When does an object speed up?
   **Answer:** When acceleration is in the same direction as its velocity.

## Lesson 2 — Find the resultant of coplanar forces

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain how to find the resultant of several forces acting in the same plane by adding force vectors.  
**Starter:** Use force arrows acting on a box. Students draw a single arrow for the resultant force.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a resultant force?
   **Answer:** The single force that has the same effect as the combined forces.
2. Two forces of 5 N and 3 N act in the same direction. What is the resultant?
   **Answer:** 8 N in that direction.
3. Two forces of 5 N and 3 N act in opposite directions. What is the resultant?
   **Answer:** 2 N in the direction of the 5 N force.

## Lesson 3 — Relate acceleration to resultant force and mass

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Analyse how an object's acceleration depends on the resultant force and its mass, applying Newton's second law.  
**Starter:** Compare pushing an empty trolley and a loaded trolley with the same force.

**Formative practice**
1. State Newton's second law in equation form.
   **Answer:** F = ma for the resultant force.
2. What happens to acceleration if the same net force acts on a larger mass?
   **Answer:** Acceleration decreases.
3. A 2 kg object experiences a net force of 10 N. What is its acceleration?
   **Answer:** 5 m/s².

## Lesson 4 — Explain action-reaction force pairs

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain that interaction forces occur in equal and opposite pairs, act simultaneously and act on different objects.  
**Starter:** Ask students to identify the two interacting objects when they push a wall or jump from the floor.

**Formative practice**
1. Do action and reaction forces act on the same object?
   **Answer:** No. They act on two different interacting objects.
2. A swimmer pushes water backward. What is the reaction force?
   **Answer:** The water pushes the swimmer forward.
3. Are action and reaction forces equal in magnitude?
   **Answer:** Yes, they are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.

## Lesson 5 — Explain motion under different types of acceleration

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how acceleration produces straight-line, projectile, circular and oscillatory motion.  
**Starter:** Show examples of a thrown ball, a swinging pendulum and a turning object.

**Formative practice**
1. What type of motion does an object follow when projected near Earth's surface, ignoring air resistance?
   **Answer:** Projectile motion along a curved path.
2. What provides the inward acceleration in uniform circular motion?
   **Answer:** A centripetal resultant force directed toward the centre.
3. Where is the equilibrium position in simple oscillation?
   **Answer:** The position where the resultant force is zero.

## Lesson 6 — Explain gravity and orbital motion

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/6 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how gravitational attraction acts on objects with mass and relates to the motion of satellites and the Moon around Earth.  
**Starter:** Use a diagram of the Moon orbiting Earth to discuss the role of gravity.

**Formative practice**
1. What causes the Moon to remain in orbit around Earth?
   **Answer:** Earth's gravitational attraction provides the centripetal force.
2. Does gravity act on an orbiting satellite?
   **Answer:** Yes. Gravity continually changes its direction of motion.
3. Why does a satellite not simply fall straight down to Earth?
   **Answer:** It has sufficient sideways velocity while gravity continually bends its path.

## Lesson 7 — Explain magnetic fields produced by electric current

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-07`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/7 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain that an electric current produces a magnetic field around its path, using the right-hand rule to determine direction.  
**Starter:** Place a compass near a current-carrying wire in a safe demonstration or use a simulation.

**Formative practice**
1. What happens around a wire when electric current flows?
   **Answer:** A magnetic field is produced around the wire.
2. What rule can help determine the direction of the magnetic field?
   **Answer:** The right-hand grip rule.
3. What happens to the magnetic field if the current increases?
   **Answer:** Its strength increases, for the same wire arrangement.

## Lesson 8 — Explain magnetic forces and electric motors

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-08`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/8 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain magnetic forces on moving charged particles and current-carrying conductors in a magnetic field, and relate these forces to motor operation.  
**Starter:** Show a simple motor diagram and ask students where the magnetic force acts.

**Formative practice**
1. When can a moving charged particle experience a magnetic force?
   **Answer:** When it moves with a component perpendicular to the magnetic field.
2. What happens to a current-carrying wire placed across a magnetic field?
   **Answer:** It experiences a magnetic force.
3. What is the basic principle of an electric motor?
   **Answer:** A current-carrying conductor experiences a force in a magnetic field, producing rotation.

## Lesson 9 — Explain electromagnetic induction and EMF

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/9 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how a changing magnetic field through a conductor coil induces an electromotive force and give a practical application.  
**Starter:** Move a magnet into and out of a coil in a safe demonstration or simulation.

**Formative practice**
1. What is electromagnetic induction?
   **Answer:** The production of an EMF when magnetic flux through a conductor changes.
2. What is one device based on electromagnetic induction?
   **Answer:** An electric generator.
3. How can the induced EMF be increased in a simple coil setup?
   **Answer:** By changing magnetic flux more rapidly, such as moving the magnet faster or increasing the number of turns.

## Lesson 10 — Explain strong and weak nuclear forces

**Objective:** `M5-S2_2-10`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.2 ม.5/10 | **Priority:** Should-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research and explain the roles of the strong and weak nuclear forces in nuclear stability and radioactive beta decay.  
**Starter:** Use a simple nuclear diagram to distinguish strong and weak interactions.

**Formative practice**
1. What does the strong nuclear force help do?
   **Answer:** It binds particles in the nucleus and contributes to nuclear stability.
2. Which interaction is involved in beta decay?
   **Answer:** The weak nuclear interaction.
3. Are the strong and weak nuclear forces the same as gravity?
   **Answer:** No. They are distinct fundamental interactions.

## Lesson 11 — Explain nuclear energy, fission and fusion

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-01`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/1 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain nuclear energy from fission and fusion and relate released energy to changes in mass.  
**Starter:** Compare diagrams of a heavy nucleus splitting and light nuclei combining.

**Formative practice**
1. What happens in nuclear fission?
   **Answer:** A heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei and releases energy.
2. What happens in nuclear fusion?
   **Answer:** Light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus and release energy.
3. What relationship connects mass change and energy?
   **Answer:** E = mc².

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-02`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/2 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research how alternative energy sources are converted into electrical energy and discuss the efficiency and cost-effectiveness of technologies.  
**Starter:** Compare solar panels and a power station using efficiency and cost.

**Formative practice**
1. How does a solar cell produce electricity?
   **Answer:** It converts light energy directly into electrical energy.
2. How can a nuclear power station generate electricity?
   **Answer:** Nuclear energy heats a working fluid to drive a turbine and generator.
3. Name two factors for comparing energy technologies.
   **Answer:** Efficiency and cost; environmental impact and reliability are also relevant.

## Lesson 13 — Explain reflection, refraction, diffraction and superposition

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-03`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/3 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain reflection, refraction, diffraction and superposition when waves encounter boundaries, obstacles or other waves.  
**Starter:** Use a ripple tank video to identify reflection, refraction, diffraction and overlapping waves.

**Formative practice**
1. What is reflection of a wave?
   **Answer:** A wave bounces back after meeting a boundary or obstacle.
2. What is refraction?
   **Answer:** A change in wave direction due to a change in speed when entering another medium.
3. What happens during superposition?
   **Answer:** Two or more waves overlap and their displacements combine.

## Lesson 14 — Explain natural frequency and resonance

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-04`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/4 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain natural frequency, resonance and the increased amplitude that can occur when a periodic driving force matches an object's natural frequency.  
**Starter:** Show a playground swing and discuss what happens when pushes match its natural rhythm.

**Formative practice**
1. What is natural frequency?
   **Answer:** The frequency at which an object naturally oscillates after being disturbed.
2. What is resonance?
   **Answer:** A large response when a driving frequency matches or is close to the natural frequency.
3. Give one real-world example of resonance.
   **Answer:** A musical instrument resonance or vibration of a bridge/building.

## Lesson 15 — Explain wave behaviour of sound

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-05`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/5 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain reflection, refraction, diffraction and superposition of sound waves.  
**Starter:** Use an echo recording and a sound-wave animation.

**Formative practice**
1. What causes an echo?
   **Answer:** Sound reflection from a surface returning to the listener.
2. Can sound diffract around an obstacle?
   **Answer:** Yes, sound can spread around edges and through openings.
3. What happens when two sound waves overlap?
   **Answer:** They superpose and may produce constructive or destructive interference.

## Lesson 16 — Relate sound intensity, loudness and frequency to hearing

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-06`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/6 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain the relationship between sound intensity and sound level, and how frequency relates to pitch and human hearing.  
**Starter:** Compare a low-pitch and high-pitch tone and discuss safe listening levels.

**Formative practice**
1. What does frequency mainly affect in our perception of sound?
   **Answer:** Pitch: higher frequency is heard as a higher pitch.
2. What does sound intensity relate to?
   **Answer:** The sound energy passing through a unit area per unit time.
3. What is the approximate human hearing frequency range?
   **Answer:** About 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz for a typical young healthy person.

## Lesson 17 — Explain echo, beats, Doppler effect and sound resonance

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-07`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/7 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain echoes, beats from nearby frequencies, Doppler frequency shifts and resonance in air columns.  
**Starter:** Play two nearby tones and demonstrate an echo or moving sound source.

**Formative practice**
1. What produces beats?
   **Answer:** The superposition of two sound waves with slightly different frequencies.
2. What is the Doppler effect?
   **Answer:** A perceived change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.
3. How can resonance occur in an air column?
   **Answer:** When the sound frequency matches a natural frequency of the air column.

**Learning outcome:** Research and give examples of using sound knowledge in medicine, musical tuning, instrument design and human voice production.

   **Answer:** Medical imaging, such as viewing a developing baby or internal organs.

## Lesson 19 — Explain colour vision and colour-vision deficiency

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-09`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/9 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how objects absorb and reflect different colours of light and how cone-cell differences can cause colour-vision deficiency.  
**Starter:** Use coloured objects under white and coloured light to discuss reflected light.

**Formative practice**
1. Why does a red object look red under white light?
   **Answer:** It reflects more red light and absorbs more of other colours.
2. Which cells in the retina help detect colour?
   **Answer:** Cone cells.
3. What can cause colour-vision deficiency?
   **Answer:** Differences or defects in cone photoreceptors or their visual pigments.

## Lesson 20 — Explain colour filters and mixing light and pigments

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-10`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/10 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Observe and explain how colour filters transmit or absorb light and compare additive mixing of light with subtractive mixing of pigments.  
**Starter:** Mix red, green and blue light in a simulation, then compare with mixing paints.

**Formative practice**
1. What does a red filter do to white light?
   **Answer:** It transmits red light and absorbs or blocks much of the other colours.
2. What do red, green and blue light combine to make in suitable proportions?
   **Answer:** White light.
3. What do cyan, magenta and yellow pigments ideally combine to make?
   **Answer:** A very dark colour, ideally black.

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-11`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/11 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Research electromagnetic waves, their electric and magnetic field components, and the operation of devices that use them.  
**Starter:** Show an electromagnetic spectrum diagram and everyday devices that use different bands.

**Formative practice**
1. What are the two changing fields in an electromagnetic wave?
   **Answer:** Electric and magnetic fields.
2. How are these fields oriented relative to one another and the direction of travel?
   **Answer:** They are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.
3. Name one device that uses electromagnetic waves.
   **Answer:** A remote control, X-ray imaging device, radio or MRI system.

## Lesson 22 — Explain communication using electromagnetic signals

**Objective:** `M5-S2_3-12`  
**Official indicator:** ว 2.3 ม.5/12 | **Priority:** Must-Know  
**Learning outcome:** Explain how electromagnetic waves transmit information and compare analogue and digital communication signals.  
**Starter:** Compare a radio broadcast and a digital message to discuss how information is encoded and sent.

**Formative practice**
1. What is a signal used for in communication?
   **Answer:** It carries encoded information from a sender to a receiver.
2. What is one difference between analogue and digital signals?
   **Answer:** Analogue signals vary continuously; digital signals use discrete values.
3. Why can digital transmission be more resistant to errors?
   **Answer:** It can be regenerated and checked, reducing the accumulation of noise and distortion.
