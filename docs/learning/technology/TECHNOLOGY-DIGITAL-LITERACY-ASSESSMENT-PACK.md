## Lesson 1 — M1: Safe and responsible use of information technology

**Objective:** `M1-W4_2-04` Use information technology safely and follow source rules

**Learning outcome:** Use classroom technology safely, protect personal information and follow the rules attached to information and media sources.

**Teaching activity:** Give students fictional examples of a class profile, a shared password, a photograph and a copied online picture. Students sort each action into safe, unsafe or needs permission, then explain a safer choice. Use fictional names and data only.

**Independent practice**

1. A website asks a student to post their home address and phone number to join a game. What should the student do, and why?
   **Answer:** Do not post the information. Ask a trusted teacher or parent and use the service only if it is approved; a home address and phone number are private information.
2. A classmate offers to share their password so you can finish an assignment. Explain the safe response.
   **Answer:** Do not use or share another person's password. Use your own account and ask the teacher for access help.
3. You want to use a picture from a website in a school poster. What should you check before using it?
   **Answer:** Check the licence or permission, follow the stated conditions, and credit the creator where required; otherwise choose an image that is permitted for reuse.

## Lesson 2 — M2: Digital responsibility, privacy and intellectual property

**Objective:** `M2-W4_2-04` Use information technology responsibly and respect intellectual property

**Learning outcome:** Make responsible choices when publishing, sharing and reusing digital work, while protecting personal information and respecting creators' rights.

**Teaching activity:** Present fictional scenarios about reposting a classmate's photo, copying a graphic and publishing a group project. Students identify permission, attribution and privacy checks before sharing.

**Independent practice**

1. A student wants to upload a group photograph. One classmate does not want their face shown. What should happen before the photograph is published?
   **Answer:** Respect the classmate's choice. Obtain permission from everyone shown or remove/cover the person before publishing, following school rules.
2. A student copies an entire online infographic and removes the creator's name. Explain two problems with this action.
   **Answer:** It misrepresents someone else's work and fails to respect copyright or attribution requirements. The student should use permitted material, credit the creator and add their own work.
3. A public discussion becomes insulting and includes a student's private details. What responsible actions should a witness take?
   **Answer:** Do not forward or amplify it; preserve evidence only as school policy allows, report it to a trusted teacher or platform moderator, and protect the student's privacy.

## Lesson 3 — M3: Evaluate information reliability and misinformation

**Objective:** `M3-W4_2-03` Evaluate information reliability and media misinformation

**Learning outcome:** Check who created information, when it was published, what evidence supports it and whether independent reliable sources confirm it.

**Teaching activity:** Compare two fictional posts making different claims about a school closure. Students check the author, date, evidence, original announcement and independent confirmation before deciding what to trust.

**Independent practice**

1. A viral post says that all schools in a province are closed tomorrow, but it gives no source or date. Describe three checks before sharing it.
   **Answer:** Check the original official school or government announcement, verify the publication date and location, and compare the claim with another reliable independent source.
2. A video shows a real event but its caption claims it happened yesterday in Thailand. What evidence could help test the caption?
   **Answer:** Check the original uploader and date, locate the earliest available version, examine credible reporting and verify the place and time using independent evidence. A real video can have a false caption.
3. A website gives a surprising health or science claim and links only to an anonymous social-media comment. How should a student evaluate it?
   **Answer:** Treat it as unverified, look for named qualified sources and original evidence, compare reputable independent sources, and avoid sharing it as fact until supported.

## Lesson 4 — M3: Safe technology use, computer law and copyright

**Objective:** `M3-W4_2-04` Use technology safely and comply with computer law and copyright

**Learning outcome:** Use digital systems responsibly, avoid harmful or unlawful online actions and respect the rights of creators.

**Teaching activity:** Use fictional case cards involving account access, harmful messages, software downloads and copied music. Students identify risks, school rules and lawful alternatives.

**Independent practice**

1. A student discovers a classmate's account is still signed in on a shared computer. What should the student do?
   **Answer:** Do not open, copy or change anything. Sign out or tell the account owner or teacher so the account can be secured.
2. Someone sends a threatening message and asks the recipient to forward it to the whole class. What is a safer response?
   **Answer:** Do not forward or retaliate. Keep evidence in line with school guidance, block or report the sender where appropriate, and tell a trusted adult or school authority.
3. A student wants background music for a public school video. Give two lawful ways to obtain music.
   **Answer:** Use music with a licence that permits the intended use and follow its conditions, obtain permission from the rights holder, or choose original music the student has created.

## Lesson 5 — M4: Responsible digital practice in an integrated project

**Objective:** `M4-W4_2-01` Apply computational thinking in an integrated project

**Learning outcome:** Plan a real-life digital project that solves a problem while considering privacy, security, accessibility, reliable information and responsible sharing.

**Teaching activity:** Teams design a simple school-resource finder using fictional sample data. Before planning the algorithm, they identify what data is necessary, what must not be collected, how information will be checked and who may access the result.

**Independent practice**

1. Your team is designing a school club finder. Which student details should the prototype avoid collecting, and why?
   **Answer:** Avoid unnecessary sensitive or identifying details such as home addresses, personal phone numbers, passwords or private account data. Collect only information needed for the stated purpose.
2. The project displays opening times copied from an old social-media post. What should the team do before publishing the information?
   **Answer:** Verify the information against a current authoritative source, record its date/source and correct or remove unverified details.
3. A team wants to publish its project for the whole school. Name two safeguards it should check before release.
   **Answer:** Check that no personal or confidential data is exposed and that access/security settings are appropriate; also check permissions for any reused content and make the interface usable by its intended audience.

## Lesson 6 — M5: Ethical data science and responsible digital services

**Objective:** `M5-W4_2-01` Use data science to solve problems and create value

**Learning outcome:** Collect and analyse appropriate data to solve a real-life problem while considering consent, data minimisation, bias, security and fair communication of results.

**Teaching activity:** Use a fictional, anonymous survey about school library preferences. Students identify the purpose, decide which questions are necessary, look for sampling bias and plan how to report results without identifying respondents.

**Independent practice**

1. A team wants to survey students about library opening times. Give two ways to collect useful data while respecting privacy.
   **Answer:** Explain the purpose and obtain appropriate consent; ask only relevant questions and avoid names or identifying details unless genuinely necessary and authorised.
2. A survey is answered only by students who visit the library every day. Why might the results be biased, and what could improve the sample?
   **Answer:** The sample excludes students who rarely visit, so it may not represent all students. Invite a broader, fair sample across relevant student groups without pressuring participation.
3. A chart is based on a small sample but is presented as proof that every student has the same opinion. What should the report say instead?
   **Answer:** State the sample size and how it was collected, describe the result as limited to those responses, explain uncertainty or limitations, and avoid claiming it represents everyone.

## Lesson 7 — M6: Ethical information sharing and technology's social impact

**Objective:** `M6-W4_2-01` Share information safely and analyse technology's social impact

**Learning outcome:** Present and share information safely and ethically, and critically analyse how technological change affects people, work, society and culture.

**Teaching activity:** Students assess a fictional proposal to use an automated system to recommend school activities. They consider data privacy, accuracy, accessibility, human oversight, possible bias and effects on different groups before recommending safeguards.

**Independent practice**

1. A student prepares a public presentation using a dataset that contains names and personal comments. What should be done before sharing the presentation?
   **Answer:** Remove or anonymise identifying details, check permission and the purpose for sharing, limit access where needed, and follow school privacy rules.
2. An automated recommendation system gives fewer opportunities to one group of students. What should the school investigate before relying on its recommendations?
   **Answer:** Check the data and criteria used, test outcomes across different groups, investigate possible bias or missing data, provide human review and allow decisions to be challenged.
3. Describe one possible benefit and one possible risk of automation changing jobs in a community. Suggest a responsible response.
   **Answer:** A benefit may be safer or more efficient work; a risk may be job displacement or unequal access to new opportunities. Responses can include training, accessible support, transparent decisions and involving affected people in planning.

## Teacher safety and assessment guidance

- These questions use fictional scenarios. Never ask students to submit passwords, private messages, home addresses, personal contact details or real account credentials.
- Do not ask students to upload identifiable photographs or personal datasets as evidence for these questions.
- Short answers are imported as manual-review items. Teachers must review the protected answer key and apply the published marking criteria; accept equivalent correct explanations rather than requiring exact wording.
- For questions about laws, school policy or platform rules, verify the current local requirements before teaching or marking. This pack is an educational resource, not legal advice.
- Publish only after confirming the selected Technology curriculum, grade and objective mapping in the question bank.
