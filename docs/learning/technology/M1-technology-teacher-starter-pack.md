# M1 Technology — Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education — Technology M1–M6  
**Subject:** TECH — Technology  
**Grade:** M1 (Mathayom 1)  
**Curriculum version:** B.E. 2551 (2008), revised 2017  
**Coverage:** 9 official indicators across Design & Technology (ว 4.1) and Computing Science (ว 4.2).

This pack contains one starter lesson for every official indicator allocated to this grade. Each lesson includes a teaching activity and three practice questions with answer keys. It is a teacher planning resource, not published app content.

## Teacher safety and assessment guidance

Use school-approved, teacher-supervised materials and tools for design activities. Do not use mains electricity, exposed high-voltage components, hazardous chemicals, uncontrolled heat sources or unsafe cutting tools in student activities. Prefer simulations, low-voltage kits and prepared data when appropriate. For Computing Science, use fictional or anonymised datasets; never ask students to share passwords, private messages, addresses or other personal information. Require students to check source reliability, respect intellectual property and follow school and applicable computer-use rules. Review every lesson for local school resources, accessibility and age suitability before publishing.

## Lesson starters

### Lesson 1 — Explain technology concepts and factors that change technology

**Objective:** `M1-W4_1-01` — Explain technology concepts and factors that change technology  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.1/1  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Explain technology concepts and factors that change technology  
**Teaching activity:** Students sort everyday examples into natural objects and human-made technologies, then trace how one item changed over time.

**Independent practice**
1. What makes an object a technology?  
   **Answer:** It is created or adapted by people to meet a need or solve a problem.
2. Name one factor that can cause technology to change.  
   **Answer:** New knowledge, user needs, available resources, cost, safety or environmental concerns.
3. Why should people consider the effects of a new technology?  
   **Answer:** It can affect people, society and the environment.

### Lesson 2 — Identify everyday problems and gather relevant information

**Objective:** `M1-W4_1-02` — Identify everyday problems and gather relevant information  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.1/2  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Identify everyday problems and gather relevant information  
**Teaching activity:** Use a school scenario such as a crowded water station. Students identify users, observe the problem and record relevant evidence.

**Independent practice**
1. What is a problem statement?  
   **Answer:** A clear description of a need or difficulty that should be solved.
2. Name two useful sources of information about a school problem.  
   **Answer:** Observation, interviews, surveys, measurements or reliable documents.
3. Why collect evidence before choosing a solution?  
   **Answer:** It helps ensure the solution addresses the real problem rather than an assumption.

### Lesson 3 — Design and plan a solution to an everyday problem

**Objective:** `M1-W4_1-03` — Design and plan a solution to an everyday problem  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.1/3  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Design and plan a solution to an everyday problem  
**Teaching activity:** Teams sketch two possible solutions to a classroom storage problem, compare them against criteria and plan the chosen design.

**Independent practice**
1. What is a design criterion?  
   **Answer:** A feature used to judge whether a solution meets the need.
2. What is a constraint?  
   **Answer:** A limit such as time, budget, size or available materials.
3. Why compare more than one design?  
   **Answer:** To select the option that best meets the criteria and constraints.

### Lesson 4 — Test, evaluate and improve a solution

**Objective:** `M1-W4_1-04` — Test, evaluate and improve a solution  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.1/4  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Test, evaluate and improve a solution  
**Teaching activity:** Students test a paper bridge with equal-weight counters, record results, identify a weakness and revise the design.

**Independent practice**
1. What should a fair test keep the same?  
   **Answer:** All conditions except the feature being tested.
2. What is a design defect?  
   **Answer:** A weakness that prevents the solution from meeting a criterion.
3. Why test a revised design again?  
   **Answer:** To check whether the change improved performance and did not create another problem.

### Lesson 5 — Use materials, tools and mechanisms safely

**Objective:** `M1-W4_1-05` — Use materials, tools and mechanisms safely  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.1/5  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Use materials, tools and mechanisms safely  
**Teaching activity:** Demonstrate safe selection and handling of classroom materials and hand tools; compare a simple lever and pulley model.

**Independent practice**
1. What should guide the choice of a tool?  
   **Answer:** The task, material, tool capability and safety requirements.
2. Why inspect a tool before use?  
   **Answer:** To identify damage or faults that could cause injury or poor results.
3. Name one safe practice when using a mechanism or electrical kit.  
   **Answer:** Follow instructions, keep hands clear of moving parts and use only low-voltage school kits under supervision.

### Lesson 6 — Design abstract algorithms for real-life problems

**Objective:** `M1-W4_2-01` — Design abstract algorithms for real-life problems  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.1/1  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Design abstract algorithms for real-life problems  
**Teaching activity:** Students describe a classroom routine, remove irrelevant details and represent the essential steps with a flowchart.

**Independent practice**
1. What is an algorithm?  
   **Answer:** A clear ordered set of steps for solving a problem or completing a task.
2. What does abstraction mean?  
   **Answer:** Focusing on the important details and ignoring irrelevant information.
3. Why use a flowchart?  
   **Answer:** It shows the sequence and decisions in an algorithm visually.

### Lesson 7 — Design and write simple programs for mathematics or science

**Objective:** `M1-W4_2-02` — Design and write simple programs for mathematics or science  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.1/2  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Design and write simple programs for mathematics or science  
**Teaching activity:** Use a block-based or text-based environment to calculate the area of a rectangle from length and width; test several values.

**Independent practice**
1. What are the inputs for a rectangle-area program?  
   **Answer:** Length and width.
2. What formula should the program calculate?  
   **Answer:** Area = length × width.
3. Why test a program with different values?  
   **Answer:** To check that it works correctly for more than one case.

### Lesson 8 — Collect, process and present primary data

**Objective:** `M1-W4_2-03` — Collect, process and present primary data  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.1/3  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Collect, process and present primary data  
**Teaching activity:** Students conduct a short anonymous class survey about preferred reading formats, tally results and create a bar chart.

**Independent practice**
1. What is primary data?  
   **Answer:** Data collected directly for the investigation.
2. Why check data for missing or duplicate entries?  
   **Answer:** To improve accuracy before processing and presenting it.
3. Which chart is suitable for comparing categories?  
   **Answer:** A bar chart.

### Lesson 9 — Use information technology safely and follow source rules

**Objective:** `M1-W4_2-04` — Use information technology safely and follow source rules  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.1/4  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Use information technology safely and follow source rules  
**Teaching activity:** Discuss a fictional online account scenario; identify personal information, safe source use and how to report harmful content.

**Independent practice**
1. Give two examples of personal information.  
   **Answer:** A home address and a phone number; a full name or school ID can also identify someone.
2. Why should students follow the terms for using a website or image?  
   **Answer:** To respect rules, privacy, ownership and permitted use.
3. What should a student do after receiving harmful or suspicious content?  
   **Answer:** Do not forward it; save relevant evidence safely and report it to a trusted teacher or responsible adult.
