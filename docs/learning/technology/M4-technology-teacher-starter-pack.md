# M4 Technology — Teacher Starter Pack

**Curriculum:** Thai Basic Education — Technology M1–M6  
**Subject:** TECH — Technology  
**Grade:** M4 (Mathayom 4)  
**Curriculum version:** B.E. 2551 (2008), revised 2017  
**Coverage:** 6 official indicators across Design & Technology (ว 4.1) and Computing Science (ว 4.2).

This pack contains one starter lesson for every official indicator allocated to this grade. Each lesson includes a teaching activity and three practice questions with answer keys. It is a teacher planning resource, not published app content.

## Teacher safety and assessment guidance

Use school-approved, teacher-supervised materials and tools for design activities. Do not use mains electricity, exposed high-voltage components, hazardous chemicals, uncontrolled heat sources or unsafe cutting tools in student activities. Prefer simulations, low-voltage kits and prepared data when appropriate. For Computing Science, use fictional or anonymised datasets; never ask students to share passwords, private messages, addresses or other personal information. Require students to check source reliability, respect intellectual property and follow school and applicable computer-use rules. Review every lesson for local school resources, accessibility and age suitability before publishing.

## Lesson starters

## Lesson 1 — Analyse core technology concepts and their impacts

**Objective:** `M4-W4_1-01` — Analyse core technology concepts and their impacts  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/1  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Analyse core technology concepts and their impacts  
**Teaching activity:** Students analyse a complex product system and map its links with science, mathematics, society, economy and environment.

**Independent practice**
1. What is a technological system?  
   **Answer:** A set of connected parts that work together to achieve a purpose.
2. Why assess social and environmental impacts before developing technology?  
   **Answer:** To identify benefits, risks and unintended consequences before implementation.
3. Name one way science and mathematics influence technology.  
   **Answer:** They provide principles, measurements and models used in design and testing.

## Lesson 2 — Define complex problems that affect society

**Objective:** `M4-W4_1-02` — Define complex problems that affect society  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/2  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Define complex problems that affect society  
**Teaching activity:** Students frame a societal challenge such as urban heat or food waste using stakeholder needs and supplied evidence.

**Independent practice**
1. What makes a problem complex?  
   **Answer:** It has interacting causes, stakeholders, constraints or competing goals.
2. Why analyse stakeholder needs?  
   **Answer:** Different groups may experience the problem and solution differently.
3. What should a good problem definition include?  
   **Answer:** Context, affected users, evidence, constraints and the intended outcome.

## Lesson 3 — Design solutions with software and available resources

**Objective:** `M4-W4_1-03` — Design solutions with software and available resources  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/3  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Design solutions with software and available resources  
**Teaching activity:** Students create a digital concept model, compare alternatives and plan a prototype within resource constraints.

**Independent practice**
1. How can software support design?  
   **Answer:** It can model, draw, simulate, calculate or document a design.
2. Why compare alternatives before building?  
   **Answer:** It reduces waste and helps select a feasible solution.
3. What should a prototype plan specify?  
   **Answer:** Design requirements, materials, tools, sequence, roles and safety controls.

## Lesson 4 — Test, analyse and improve solutions for further development

**Objective:** `M4-W4_1-04` — Test, analyse and improve solutions for further development  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/4  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Test, analyse and improve solutions for further development  
**Teaching activity:** Students design a test protocol, analyse prototype data and propose a justified second iteration.

**Independent practice**
1. What is a performance criterion?  
   **Answer:** A measurable or observable requirement used to judge a solution.
2. Why analyse the cause of a defect rather than only describing it?  
   **Answer:** Understanding the cause helps choose an effective improvement.
3. What evidence supports a recommendation for further development?  
   **Answer:** Repeatable test results, user feedback and comparison against requirements.

## Lesson 5 — Use advanced materials, mechanisms and electrical technologies safely

**Objective:** `M4-W4_1-05` — Use advanced materials, mechanisms and electrical technologies safely  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.1 ม.4/5  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Use advanced materials, mechanisms and electrical technologies safely  
**Teaching activity:** Students evaluate material and component choices for a supervised engineering model using datasheets and risk controls.

**Independent practice**
1. Why consult a component datasheet?  
   **Answer:** It provides ratings, operating limits, connections and safety information.
2. What is a safe approach to electrical prototyping at school?  
   **Answer:** Use approved low-voltage supplies, inspect components and follow teacher-approved procedures.
3. Why consider material properties in a design?  
   **Answer:** Strength, weight, durability, conductivity and cost affect performance and safety.

## Lesson 6 — Apply computational thinking in an integrated project

**Objective:** `M4-W4_2-01` — Apply computational thinking in an integrated project  
**Official indicator:** ว 4.2 ม.4/1  
**Suggested duration:** 45–60 minutes  
**Learning outcome:** Apply computational thinking in an integrated project  
**Teaching activity:** Students plan an interdisciplinary project, define inputs and outputs, develop an algorithm and create a tested prototype or simulation.

**Independent practice**
1. What are the main stages of computational thinking?  
   **Answer:** Decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction and algorithm design.
2. Why define inputs and outputs before coding?  
   **Answer:** They clarify what information the solution needs and what result it must produce.
3. How can a project be tested?  
   **Answer:** Use planned test cases and compare actual outputs with expected results.
