# M4 lesson starters

## Lesson 1 — Sets and elementary logic
**Objective:** M4-K1_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students describe sets, use set notation, perform basic set operations and interpret simple logical statements.

**Teaching sequence:** Begin with groups of classroom objects; introduce set, element, subset and universal set; use Venn diagrams for union and intersection; connect statements with and/or/not and simple truth values.

**Practice and answers**
1. If A = {1, 2, 3} and B = {3, 4}, find A ∩ B. **Answer:** {3}.
2. Using the same sets, find A ∪ B. **Answer:** {1, 2, 3, 4}.
3. Is the statement “5 is an element of {2, 4, 6}” true or false? **Answer:** False.

## Lesson 2 — Counting principles, permutations and combinations
**Objective:** M4-K3_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students use the addition and multiplication principles and distinguish arrangements from selections.

**Teaching sequence:** Make a tree diagram for a simple choice; compare “one option or another” with “one choice followed by another”; introduce factorial notation; compare order-sensitive permutations with order-insensitive combinations.

**Practice and answers**
1. A student has 3 shirts and 2 pairs of trousers. How many different outfits are possible? **Answer:** 3 × 2 = 6.
2. How many ways can 3 different books be arranged on a shelf? **Answer:** 3! = 6.
3. How many ways can 2 students be selected from 5 students? **Answer:** 5C2 = 10.

## Lesson 3 — Probability and applications
**Objective:** M4-K3_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students calculate probabilities and use probability rules in simple situations.

**Teaching sequence:** List sample spaces; distinguish mutually exclusive and independent events; model complements; solve problems using equally likely outcomes and check that probabilities lie between 0 and 1.

**Practice and answers**
1. A fair die is rolled. Find the probability of getting a number greater than 4. **Answer:** 2/6 = 1/3.
2. A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of getting two heads. **Answer:** 1/4.
3. If P(A) = 0.7, find P(not A). **Answer:** 0.3.

# M5 lesson starters

## Lesson 4 — Real numbers, radicals and rational exponents
**Objective:** M5-K1_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students simplify expressions involving radicals and rational exponents and apply real-number properties.

**Teaching sequence:** Review index laws; connect fractional exponents to roots; simplify radicals; practise operations and comparisons; discuss restrictions where expressions are defined over the real numbers.

**Practice and answers**
1. Evaluate 16^(1/2). **Answer:** 4.
2. Simplify 8^(2/3). **Answer:** 4.
3. Simplify √50. **Answer:** 5√2.

## Lesson 5 — Functions and graphs
**Objective:** M5-K1_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students interpret a function as an input-output relationship and use its graph to describe a situation.

**Teaching sequence:** Build a function table; identify inputs and outputs; plot ordered pairs; interpret intercepts and changes; discuss domain and range in a contextual example.

**Practice and answers**
1. If f(x) = 2x + 3, find f(4). **Answer:** 11.
2. For y = 3x − 6, find the x-intercept. **Answer:** (2, 0).
3. A tank contains 20 litres and loses 2 litres each minute. Write a function for the amount of water A after t minutes. **Answer:** A(t) = 20 − 2t, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10.

## Lesson 6 — Sequences and series
**Objective:** M5-K1_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify arithmetic and geometric sequences and calculate terms and simple sums.

**Teaching sequence:** Look for constant differences and ratios; write nth-term rules; compare arithmetic and geometric growth; connect a sequence to its partial sum.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the next term: 4, 7, 10, 13, … **Answer:** 16.
2. Find the 10th term of the arithmetic sequence 2, 5, 8, 11, … **Answer:** 29.
3. Find the sum of the first 5 terms of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. **Answer:** 30.

## Lesson 7 — Interest and time value of money
**Objective:** M5-K1_3-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students calculate simple interest and understand how time and interest rates affect the future value of money.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare saving and borrowing examples; identify principal, rate and time; calculate simple interest; introduce compound growth as interest on an accumulated balance; discuss why rates and periods must use consistent units.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the simple interest on 2,000 baht at 5% per year for 2 years. **Answer:** 200 baht.
2. Find the total amount after the simple interest in Question 1. **Answer:** 2,200 baht.
3. An amount of 1,000 baht earns 10% compound interest annually for 2 years. Find the final amount. **Answer:** 1,210 baht.

# M6 lesson starters

## Lesson 8 — Statistics for decision-making
**Objective:** M6-K3_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students present data, interpret statistical measures and explain how statistics can support or mislead a decision.

**Teaching sequence:** Use a familiar decision such as comparing travel times or class results; select suitable tables and graphs; interpret mean, median, spread and relevant summary measures; discuss sample size, outliers and whether a conclusion is justified.

**Practice and answers**
1. Two classes have the same median test score of 70. Class A has scores clustered closely around 70, while Class B has a much wider spread. Which class is more consistent? **Answer:** Class A.
2. Find the mean of 60, 70, 70, 80 and 100. **Answer:** 76.
3. A survey of 8 students is used to claim that all students in a school prefer online homework. Identify one weakness. **Answer:** The sample is too small and may not represent the whole school.

## Teacher authoring and quality checks

- Create each lesson as a separate **draft** content item.
- Select curriculum **TH-MATH-M1-M6**, the matching grade (**M4**, **M5** or **M6**), subject **MATH**, and the exact objective code.
- Create question-bank items under the same curriculum, grade, subject and objective.
- These examples are a starting point, not a complete upper-secondary syllabus or assessment bank. Teachers should review calculations, notation, language and difficulty before publication.
- Confirm each school's approved course map before adding additional or elective upper-secondary topics. Do not map elective outcomes to these core indicators merely because the topic names appear similar.
