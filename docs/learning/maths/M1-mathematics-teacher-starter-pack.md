## Lesson 1 — Rational numbers

**Objective:** M1-K1_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Identify integers, fractions and terminating/repeating decimals as rational numbers.
- Represent rational numbers on a number line.
- Compare and order rational numbers.
- Explain a real-life use of rational numbers.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Ask students where they see negative numbers, fractions and decimals in daily life (temperature, money, sports scores, measurements).
2. **Explore (10 min):** Place -3, -1/2, 0, 0.75 and 2 on a number line.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Explain that a rational number can be written as a/b, where a and b are integers and b is not zero. Connect fractions and decimals.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Compare pairs using <, > or =.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students order five rational numbers.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Students explain why -0.4 is greater than -0.7.

### Practice questions
1. Which of these is not a rational number: -4, 0.25, 3/5, or √2?  
   **Answer:** √2.
2. Arrange -1.5, 0, -2, 3/4 from smallest to largest.  
   **Answer:** -2, -1.5, 0, 3/4.
3. A temperature changes from 2°C to -3°C. What is the change in temperature?  
   **Answer:** A decrease of 5°C.

## Lesson 2 — Positive integer exponents

**Objective:** M1-K1_1-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Explain a positive integer exponent as repeated multiplication.
- Evaluate powers with positive integer exponents.
- Apply basic exponent properties with positive integer exponents.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Write 2 × 2 × 2 × 2. Ask for a shorter way to write it.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Build a table for 2¹ through 2⁵.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Explain base and exponent. Introduce aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ and (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ for positive integer exponents.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Evaluate powers and simplify products with the same base.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students write repeated multiplication in index form.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Explain why 3⁴ means 3 × 3 × 3 × 3, not 3 × 4.

### Practice questions
1. Evaluate 4³.  
   **Answer:** 64.
2. Write 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 in index form.  
   **Answer:** 5⁴.
3. Simplify 2³ × 2².  
   **Answer:** 2⁵ = 32.

## Lesson 3 — Ratio, proportion and percentage

**Objective:** M1-K1_1-03  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Express and simplify ratios.
- Find an equivalent ratio.
- Solve a proportion.
- Calculate a percentage of a quantity and apply it to familiar situations.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** A drink uses 1 cup of syrup for 4 cups of water. Ask students to describe the mixture.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Represent the mixture as 1:4 and scale it to 2:8.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Model equivalent ratios, proportion tables and percentage as “out of 100”.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Solve a recipe scaling problem and a simple discount problem.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students create one ratio from classroom objects.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Find 15% of 200.

### Practice questions
1. Simplify the ratio 12:18.  
   **Answer:** 2:3.
2. If 3 notebooks cost 45 baht, how much do 5 notebooks cost at the same rate?  
   **Answer:** 75 baht.
3. A 400-baht item has a 10% discount. What is the discount amount and sale price?  
   **Answer:** 40 baht discount; 360 baht sale price.

## Lesson 4 — One-variable linear equations

**Objective:** M1-K1_3-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Identify the unknown in a simple equation.
- Use equality properties to keep both sides balanced.
- Solve one-variable linear equations.
- Check a solution by substitution.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Show a balanced scale with an unknown mass and ask how to find it.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Use a balance model for x + 3 = 8.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Explain inverse operations and doing the same operation to both sides.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Solve x - 4 = 9 and 3x = 18.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students solve one two-step equation.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Substitute the answer back into the original equation.

### Practice questions
1. Solve x + 7 = 19.  
   **Answer:** x = 12.
2. Solve 3x = 27.  
   **Answer:** x = 9.
3. Solve 2x + 5 = 17.  
   **Answer:** x = 6.

## Lesson 5 — Graphs

**Objective:** M1-K1_3-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Read coordinates on a Cartesian plane.
- Plot ordered pairs.
- Interpret information from a simple graph.
- Use a graph to answer a real-life question.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Use a classroom seating grid to introduce position.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Locate points (2, 3), (-1, 2) and (0, -2).
3. **Teach (15 min):** Identify the x-axis, y-axis, origin and ordered pairs.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Plot points from a small data table.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students write coordinates for plotted points.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Explain what the point (0, 4) tells us.

### Practice questions
1. In which quadrant is the point (-3, 2)?  
   **Answer:** Quadrant II.
2. What are the coordinates of a point 4 units right and 1 unit down from the origin?  
   **Answer:** (4, -1).
3. A graph shows distance increasing from 0 km to 12 km. What does the vertical value represent if the vertical axis is labelled distance (km)?  
   **Answer:** Distance travelled in kilometres.

## Lesson 6 — Linear relationships

**Objective:** M1-K1_3-03  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Recognise a linear relationship in a table or graph.
- Describe a constant rate of change.
- Use a simple linear rule to predict a value.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** A taxi charges a fixed starting fee plus the same amount per kilometre. Ask what changes and what stays fixed.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Complete a table for y = 2x + 1.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Connect a rule, a table, ordered pairs and a straight-line graph.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Identify the constant increase in a table.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Write a rule from a simple situation.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Find y when x = 5 for y = 3x + 2.

### Practice questions
1. Complete the table for y = 2x + 1 when x = 0, 1, 2.  
   **Answer:** y = 1, 3, 5.
2. A service costs 20 baht plus 5 baht per item. Write a rule for the total cost C for n items.  
   **Answer:** C = 20 + 5n.
3. Using C = 20 + 5n, find the cost for 6 items.  
   **Answer:** 50 baht.

## Lesson 7 — Geometric construction

**Objective:** M1-K2_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Use a ruler, compass and protractor appropriately.
- Construct a line segment of a given length.
- Construct and bisect an angle.
- Explain the steps used in a construction.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Compare a freehand drawing with a precise construction.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Demonstrate how a compass keeps a fixed radius.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Model constructing a perpendicular bisector and angle bisector.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Students follow a step-by-step construction.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students construct a given line segment and bisect it.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Students identify which construction step ensures equal distances.

### Practice questions
1. Which instrument is used to draw an arc with a fixed radius?  
   **Answer:** Compass.
2. What does a perpendicular bisector do to a line segment?  
   **Answer:** It crosses the segment at 90° and divides it into two equal parts.
3. An angle is 80°. What are the two equal angles after it is bisected?  
   **Answer:** 40° and 40°.

## Lesson 8 — Two-dimensional and three-dimensional figures

**Objective:** M1-K2_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Distinguish 2D shapes from 3D solids.
- Identify faces, edges and vertices.
- Relate a 3D object to its 2D views or nets.
- Interpret simple front, side and top views.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Show a box, can and ball. Ask students to name their shapes.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Examine a box and count faces, edges and vertices.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Compare 2D shapes, 3D solids, nets and orthographic views.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Match solids to their nets or views.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Sketch the top and front view of a simple solid.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** State one difference between a square and a cube.

### Practice questions
1. How many faces does a cube have?  
   **Answer:** 6.
2. Which 2D shape is formed by the side face of a cube?  
   **Answer:** A square.
3. How many vertices does a rectangular prism have?  
   **Answer:** 8.

## Lesson 9 — Statistics: presenting and interpreting data

**Objective:** M1-K3_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

### Learning outcomes
Students can:
- Organise a small data set.
- Choose a suitable table or graph.
- Calculate and interpret the mean, median and mode.
- Explain a conclusion supported by data.

### Lesson sequence
1. **Warm-up (5 min):** Ask students how the class travels to school and record responses.
2. **Explore (10 min):** Organise the responses in a frequency table.
3. **Teach (15 min):** Explain bar charts, line graphs and measures of central tendency.
4. **Guided practice (10 min):** Calculate mean, median and mode for a small data set.
5. **Independent practice (5 min):** Students write one conclusion from the class data.
6. **Exit ticket (5 min):** Explain why a graph title and labelled axes matter.

### Practice questions
1. Find the mean of 4, 6, 8 and 10.  
   **Answer:** 7.
2. Find the median of 3, 5, 7, 9 and 12.  
   **Answer:** 7.
3. In the data set 2, 3, 3, 4, 5, what is the mode?  
   **Answer:** 3.
