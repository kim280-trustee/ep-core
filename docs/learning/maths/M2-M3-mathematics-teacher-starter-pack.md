# M2 lesson starters

## Lesson 1 — Integer exponents
**Objective:** M2-K1_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students evaluate powers with positive, zero and negative integer exponents and use exponent laws.

**Teaching sequence:** Start with repeated multiplication; connect reciprocal powers to negative exponents; model zero exponent; practise exponent laws; finish with scientific and everyday contexts.

**Practice and answers**
1. Evaluate 2⁻³. **Answer:** 1/8.
2. Simplify 3² × 3⁻¹. **Answer:** 3.
3. Write 1/100 as a power of 10. **Answer:** 10⁻².

## Lesson 2 — Real numbers and radicals
**Objective:** M2-K1_1-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students distinguish rational and irrational numbers, locate real numbers and evaluate simple square and cube roots.

**Teaching sequence:** Sort familiar fractions, decimals and roots; introduce irrational numbers; connect square/cube roots to inverse operations; use a number line to compare values.

**Practice and answers**
1. Is √2 rational or irrational? **Answer:** Irrational.
2. Evaluate √49. **Answer:** 7.
3. Evaluate ∛(-27). **Answer:** -3.

## Lesson 3 — Polynomial operations
**Objective:** M2-K1_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify terms and coefficients and add, subtract, multiply and divide polynomials by monomials.

**Teaching sequence:** Use algebra tiles or area models; collect like terms; model multiplication with the distributive property; practise division term by term.

**Practice and answers**
1. Simplify (2x + 3) + (x − 5). **Answer:** 3x − 2.
2. Expand 2x(3x − 4). **Answer:** 6x² − 8x.
3. Simplify 12x³ ÷ 3x. **Answer:** 4x².

## Lesson 4 — Factoring quadratic polynomials
**Objective:** M2-K1_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students factor expressions using common factors, perfect-square patterns and difference of squares.

**Teaching sequence:** Begin with common factors; use rectangle/area models for x² + bx + c; identify square patterns; compare expanded and factored forms.

**Practice and answers**
1. Factor x² + 5x + 6. **Answer:** (x + 2)(x + 3).
2. Factor 4x² − 9. **Answer:** (2x − 3)(2x + 3).
3. Factor x² + 6x + 9. **Answer:** (x + 3)².

## Lesson 5 — Surface area of prisms and cylinders
**Objective:** M2-K2_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify the surfaces of prisms and cylinders, select suitable formulas and solve practical covering problems.

**Teaching sequence:** Unfold a box and a cylinder into nets; label dimensions; derive or review surface-area formulas; solve packaging examples and include units.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 5 cm. **Answer:** 48π cm².
2. Find the total surface area of a rectangular prism measuring 2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm. **Answer:** 52 cm².
3. A cube has side length 4 cm. Find its total surface area. **Answer:** 96 cm².

## Lesson 6 — Volume of prisms and cylinders
**Objective:** M2-K2_1-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students calculate volumes and explain why the base area is multiplied by perpendicular height.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare containers; connect volume to layers of unit cubes; model prism and cylinder formulas; solve capacity problems and label cubic units.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 7 cm. **Answer:** 28π cm³.
2. A triangular prism has a triangular base with base 4 cm and height 3 cm; the prism length is 5 cm. Find its volume. **Answer:** 30 cm³.
3. Find the volume of a rectangular prism measuring 3 cm × 4 cm × 6 cm. **Answer:** 72 cm³.

## Lesson 7 — Geometric constructions
**Objective:** M2-K2_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students use a compass and straightedge to construct geometric figures and explain why the construction works.

**Teaching sequence:** Demonstrate accurate compass use; construct a perpendicular bisector; construct an angle bisector; compare paper constructions with dynamic geometry software.

**Practice and answers**
1. Which instrument draws an arc at a fixed radius? **Answer:** Compass.
2. What angle does a perpendicular bisector make with a segment? **Answer:** 90°.
3. An 80° angle is bisected. What is each new angle? **Answer:** 40°.

## Lesson 8 — Parallel lines and triangles
**Objective:** M2-K2_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify angle relationships created by parallel lines and use triangle angle properties.

**Teaching sequence:** Draw a transversal across parallel lines; identify corresponding and alternate interior angles; investigate triangle angle sums; solve missing-angle problems.

**Practice and answers**
1. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. If one corresponding angle is 65°, find the other. **Answer:** 65°.
2. A triangle has angles 50° and 60°. Find the third angle. **Answer:** 70°.
3. An exterior angle of a triangle has remote interior angles 35° and 75°. Find the exterior angle. **Answer:** 110°.

## Lesson 9 — Geometric transformations
**Objective:** M2-K2_2-03  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students describe translations, reflections and rotations and track coordinates under transformations.

**Teaching sequence:** Use grid paper; demonstrate each transformation; compare preserved lengths and angles; use coordinate rules to predict image points.

**Practice and answers**
1. Reflect (3, −2) across the x-axis. **Answer:** (3, 2).
2. Translate (1, 2) by vector (−3, 4). **Answer:** (−2, 6).
3. Rotate (2, 1) 90° anticlockwise about the origin. **Answer:** (−1, 2).

## Lesson 10 — Congruent triangles
**Objective:** M2-K2_2-04  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students recognise congruent triangles and use corresponding equal sides and angles.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare cut-out triangles; investigate SSS, SAS and ASA conditions; mark corresponding parts; justify a conclusion from given information.

**Practice and answers**
1. Which congruence condition uses three pairs of equal sides? **Answer:** SSS.
2. If two triangles are congruent and one side of the first is 7 cm, what is the corresponding side of the second? **Answer:** 7 cm.
3. Two triangles have sides 3, 4, 5 cm each. Are they congruent by SSS? **Answer:** Yes.

## Lesson 11 — Pythagorean theorem
**Objective:** M2-K2_2-05  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify the hypotenuse and use a² + b² = c² and its converse.

**Teaching sequence:** Build squares on the sides of a right triangle; identify the hypotenuse; solve missing-side problems; test whether a triangle is right-angled.

**Practice and answers**
1. A right triangle has legs 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse. **Answer:** 10 cm.
2. A right triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and one leg 5 cm. Find the other leg. **Answer:** 12 cm.
3. Is a triangle with sides 6, 8 and 10 cm right-angled? **Answer:** Yes.

## Lesson 12 — Statistics and data displays
**Objective:** M2-K3_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students select and interpret dot plots, stem-and-leaf plots and histograms and calculate measures of central tendency.

**Teaching sequence:** Collect a small class data set; organise values; choose a display; calculate mean, median and mode; interpret what the display reveals.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the mean of 2, 4, 6 and 8. **Answer:** 5.
2. Find the median of 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11. **Answer:** 7.
3. Find the mode of 2, 2, 3, 4. **Answer:** 2.

# M3 lesson starters

## Lesson 1 — Higher-degree polynomial factorization
**Objective:** M3-K1_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify factorisation patterns and factor selected polynomials of degree greater than two.

**Teaching sequence:** Review common factors and difference of squares; model sum/difference of cubes; check results by expansion; discuss when a factorisation is complete over the integers.

**Practice and answers**
1. Factor x³ + 3x² + 3x + 1. **Answer:** (x + 1)³.
2. Factor x³ − 8. **Answer:** (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4).
3. Factor x⁴ − 16. **Answer:** (x − 2)(x + 2)(x² + 4).

## Lesson 2 — Quadratic functions
**Objective:** M3-K1_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students recognise quadratic functions, identify key graph features and connect equations, tables and parabolas.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare linear and quadratic tables; plot a simple quadratic; identify the vertex and axis of symmetry; interpret roots and maximum/minimum values.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the vertex of y = x² − 4x + 3. **Answer:** (2, −1).
2. Find the roots of x² − 4x + 3 = 0. **Answer:** x = 1 and x = 3.
3. Does y = −x² have a maximum or minimum? **Answer:** A maximum at (0, 0).

## Lesson 3 — One-variable linear inequalities
**Objective:** M3-K1_3-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1 lesson of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students solve linear inequalities and represent solution sets on a number line.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare equations and inequalities; solve by inverse operations; demonstrate reversing the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative; graph solutions.

**Practice and answers**
1. Solve 2x − 3 > 5. **Answer:** x > 4.
2. Solve −2x ≤ 6. **Answer:** x ≥ −3.
3. Solve x + 4 < 10. **Answer:** x < 6.

## Lesson 4 — One-variable quadratic equations
**Objective:** M3-K1_3-02  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students solve simple quadratic equations by factorisation and square roots and check solutions.

**Teaching sequence:** Connect roots to factors; solve equations in standard form; use the zero-product property; check both roots in the original equation.

**Practice and answers**
1. Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. **Answer:** x = 2 or x = 3.
2. Solve x² = 49. **Answer:** x = 7 or x = −7.
3. Solve 2x² − 8 = 0. **Answer:** x = 2 or x = −2.

## Lesson 5 — Systems of two linear equations
**Objective:** M3-K1_3-03  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students represent two-variable situations as simultaneous equations and solve by graphing, substitution or elimination.

**Teaching sequence:** Introduce two unknown quantities; form two equations from a context; solve using elimination; verify the ordered pair in both equations.

**Practice and answers**
1. Solve x + y = 10 and x − y = 2. **Answer:** x = 6, y = 4.
2. Solve 2x + y = 11 and x + y = 7. **Answer:** x = 4, y = 3.
3. Adult tickets cost 50 baht and student tickets cost 30 baht. Four tickets cost 160 baht in total. If x is adult tickets and y is student tickets, write the total-cost equation. **Answer:** 50x + 30y = 160. (A second equation is needed to determine both quantities.)

## Lesson 6 — Surface area of pyramids, cones and spheres
**Objective:** M3-K2_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify curved and flat surfaces and apply the appropriate surface-area formulas.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare 3D models and nets; distinguish slant height from vertical height; model formulas; solve covering and packaging contexts.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the total surface area of a cone with radius 3 cm and slant height 5 cm. **Answer:** 24π cm².
2. A square pyramid has base side 4 cm and slant height 5 cm. Find its total surface area. **Answer:** 56 cm².
3. Find the surface area of a sphere with radius 3 cm. **Answer:** 36π cm².

## Lesson 7 — Volume of pyramids, cones and spheres
**Objective:** M3-K2_1-02  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students select and apply volume formulas and use correct cubic units.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare a prism with a pyramid; explore cone and cylinder relationships; model the formulas; solve capacity problems and check units.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. **Answer:** 12π cm³.
2. Find the volume of a sphere with radius 3 cm. **Answer:** 36π cm³.
3. A pyramid has base area 24 cm² and perpendicular height 6 cm. Find its volume. **Answer:** 48 cm³.

## Lesson 8 — Similar triangles
**Objective:** M3-K2_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify similar triangles and use corresponding side ratios to solve indirect measurement problems.

**Teaching sequence:** Compare scaled drawings; identify equal corresponding angles; form proportions; solve a shadow or map-scale problem and explain the ratio used.

**Practice and answers**
1. Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 2:3. A side of the smaller triangle is 6 cm. Find the matching side of the larger triangle. **Answer:** 9 cm.
2. A 1.5 m student casts a 2 m shadow. At the same time, a tree casts an 8 m shadow. Find the tree's height. **Answer:** 6 m.
3. A triangle is enlarged by scale factor 4. A side of 3 cm becomes what length? **Answer:** 12 cm.

## Lesson 9 — Trigonometric ratios
**Objective:** M3-K2_2-02  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse sides and use sine, cosine and tangent for acute angles.

**Teaching sequence:** Label sides relative to a chosen angle; introduce SOH-CAH-TOA; practise special angles; apply trigonometry to height and distance problems.

**Practice and answers**
1. What is sin 30°? **Answer:** 1/2.
2. What is tan 45°? **Answer:** 1.
3. A 10 m ladder makes a 30° angle with level ground. How high up a vertical wall does it reach? **Answer:** 5 m.

## Lesson 10 — Circle theorems
**Objective:** M3-K2_2-03  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students identify chords, arcs, tangents and angles in a circle and apply circle theorems.

**Teaching sequence:** Label circle parts; compare central and inscribed angles subtending the same arc; investigate tangent-radius relationships; solve angle problems with reasons.

**Practice and answers**
1. A central angle is 100°. Find the inscribed angle subtending the same arc. **Answer:** 50°.
2. What angle does a tangent make with the radius at the point of contact? **Answer:** 90°.
3. Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary. If one is 105°, find the opposite angle. **Answer:** 75°.

## Lesson 11 — Box plots and data analysis
**Objective:** M3-K3_1-01  
**Suggested duration:** 2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students calculate the median and quartiles, construct a box plot and interpret spread and comparison.

**Teaching sequence:** Order a data set; calculate the median and quartiles using the class's stated convention; draw the five-number summary; compare range and interquartile range; interpret the context.

**Practice and answers**
1. Find the median of 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14. **Answer:** 7.5.
2. Using the median-of-halves method, find Q1 for that data set. **Answer:** 4.5.
3. Using the same method, find Q3 and the interquartile range. **Answer:** Q3 = 11; IQR = 6.5.

## Lesson 12 — Random experiments and probability
**Objective:** M3-K3_2-01  
**Suggested duration:** 1–2 lessons of 50 minutes

**Outcomes:** Students describe sample spaces, identify events and calculate theoretical probabilities for equally likely outcomes.

**Teaching sequence:** Run coin and dice experiments; list possible outcomes; compare experimental frequency with theoretical probability; solve simple probability problems.

**Practice and answers**
1. What is the probability of getting an even number when rolling a fair six-sided die? **Answer:** 3/6 = 1/2.
2. A bag contains 3 red and 2 blue counters. What is the probability of selecting a blue counter at random? **Answer:** 2/5.
3. A fair coin is tossed once. What is the probability of tails? **Answer:** 1/2.

## Teacher authoring and quality checks

- Create each lesson as a separate **draft** content item.
- Select curriculum **TH-MATH-M1-M6**, the correct grade (**M2** or **M3**), subject **MATH**, and the exact objective code.
- Create question-bank items under the same curriculum, grade, subject and objective.
- The practice questions above are starter examples, not a complete assessment bank. A teacher should review language, mathematical accuracy, difficulty and worked solutions before publishing.
- For student-facing lessons, include worked examples, diagrams and locally familiar contexts. Use Thai explanations where needed.
- These lesson starters are not a complete term scheme of work; schools should map pacing and assessment to their approved curriculum plan.
